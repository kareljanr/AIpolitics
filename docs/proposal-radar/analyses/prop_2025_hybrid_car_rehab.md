## Executive judgment

This is the mirror image of a good reform: having paid the political cost of the 2021 greening path, Arizona **partially buys it back** for hybrids under “transition” rhetoric. Mechanism fit is poor; capture risk high. Clown 6.5 is about **incentives and lobby**, not about hating cars.

---

## Steelman

1. **Cliff risk.** Pure 2026 ICE non-deductibility may strand ordered fleets, hurt SMEs with thin EV charging access, spike costs.  
2. **Technology bridge.** PHEVs might cut CO₂ vs pure ICE in some duty cycles if actually charged.  
3. **Industrial policy.** Lease sector and auto retail employment transition smoother.  
4. **Political sustainability.** Softening keeps greening coalition alive vs full repeal pressure.

---

## Critique — why steelman fails the bar

1. **PHEV real-world emissions** often exceed lab when not charged — known EU problem. Subsidising deductibility without charge verification buys greenwash.  
2. **Cheaper transition tools exist:** short grandfather of *already ordered* vehicles; SME-only temporary regime; cash mobility budgets — not multi-year hybrid TE path (75% → 65% → 57.5% class schedules in secondary briefings).  
3. **Undoes scarcity of a rare win.** TE reform credibility dies if every cliff gets a soft rehab.  
4. **Incidence:** still a middle/high earner fringe benefit funded by general taxation.  
5. **Abundance:** keeps capital in car packaging vs wage/productivity.

### Options

A hold 2021 cliff · B narrow grandfather · C **broad hybrid rehab (this)** · D full TE kill + higher net wages · E mandate charge-data for any hybrid relief.

**A or B ≫ C.**

---

## Recommendation

**`reject`** broad rehab. If politics requires something: time-limited, order-date grandfather only, no new hybrid TE path.

**Falsifier:** independent eval shows hybrid path cuts TE **and** real emissions faster than cliff without ICE-like usage (high bar).
