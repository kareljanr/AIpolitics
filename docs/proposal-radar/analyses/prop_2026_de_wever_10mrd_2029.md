## Steelman (proponent’s best case)

Belgium’s budget problem is a path, and a one-year patch does not close a path. VRT’s account of the Monitoring Committee, read in full, says the federal government still has to find another €7.7 billion by 2029 if it wants the deficit under control. The same article says that if the government does nothing, the deficit by the end of this term goes to €38.275 billion, which that article also writes as 5.2 percent of GDP. It says the country is already in the European excessive-deficit procedure, that the deficit is 5.2 percent of GDP, and that the debt is 107.9 percent of GDP. Europe’s test, on that account, is now mainly an expenditure norm, and the political calendar treats 15 October as the day the next budget is supposed to be in Brussels. A cabinet that answers that note with a round number larger than €7.7 billion is doing the ordinary thing: the committee’s figure is a floor, measures slip, and arriving exactly on the floor means arriving short.

The prime minister’s public case, in the Terzake interview VRT published on 3 September, is that this effort will not be “solved with revenue.” He says every budget contains measures of every kind, because otherwise there is no compromise, and he also says the revenue tap will not be opened wide. He says the consolidation so far has been on the expenditure side. He says that when the tax reform is at cruising speed in 2029, taxes will sit lower than at the start of the legislature. On that telling, the €10 billion is how you stop the debt service from eating the room that a lower tax on work is supposed to create. Waiting until the interest bill itself is the policy is how a state taxes production in order to pay its creditors.

The week of 14–17 October is the steelman’s sequencing argument, and it is the reason this row exists separately from the fallback. For a few days the prime minister was described as dropping the path through 2029 and asking only for about €4 billion in 2027, with the VAT reform off the table. VRT on 15 October states that contrast in one sentence: €4 billion next year, instead of €10 billion by 2029. Partners, including CD&V’s Sammy Mahdi in the account published on 19 October, said a 2027-only deal would let the rest of the term go dark and would make 2028 and 2029 harder, and they called the fallback an attack on the index. The kern then did what that criticism asked. Its communiqué, quoted by VRT, stops the stability-law discussion for the time being and turns back to “een meerjarenbegroting met een inspanning van 10 miljard euro.” Mahdi’s line in the same piece is that you can find €10 billion without a flat VAT rise. The steelman does not need the VAT sketch. It needs the horizon.

There is also a precedent that cuts both ways and, on the proponent’s side, is a reason to try again. VRT on 2 November recalls that a year earlier the same coalition searched for €10 billion, asked the king for 50 days instead of offering a resignation, and on 24 November 2025 agreed a package reported as €9.2 billion in savings. The HLN headline on this unit says the prime minister wants to work on what failed last year. The charitable reading is that last year’s search looked impossible until it wasn’t, the debt path is still there, and a second attempt at the full horizon is the lesson. A government that will only vote what every veto allows in week one will ratify the snowball. A government that puts €10 billion back on the table after its own emergency button failed is at least pointing at the right size and the right date.

- **Metric / baseline:** From texts read in full. (1) VRT, byline 6 July 2026, URL path `2026/06/12`: the Monitoring Committee says the federal government must find an extra **€7.7 billion by 2029**. If nothing is done, the deficit by the end of the term goes to **€38.275 billion**, which that article also gives as **5.2 percent of GDP**. The same piece says the deficit is **5.2 percent of GDP** today, the worst in the euro area on its telling, debt is **107.9 percent of GDP**, Belgium is in the excessive-deficit procedure, and the operative European test is an **expenditure norm**. The next year’s budget is filed with the Commission by **15 October**. (2) VRT, 3 Sep 2026: the government aims at **€10 billion**, above that €7.7 billion floor. (3) VRT, 15 Oct 2026: the detour that week was **€4 billion for 2027 instead of €10 billion by 2029**. (4) VRT, 19 Oct 2026: the kern returns to a multi-year effort of **€10 billion**. (5) No Monitoring Committee PDF, National Bank table, debt-agency slide, or Commission recommendation was opened. A De Tijd lede of 10 July 2026 says the kern agreed to sanitise €10 billion rather than €7.7 billion by 2029 because of an interest snowball from 2031. That body was not read. The snowball year is not used as a fact.
- **Who is hurt:** Residents who will pay the deficit either as later taxes, thinner services, or a higher interest bill. Workers, if the repair is another turn of the tax on labour. Households and patients, if the repair is a health-budget squeeze whose lines are not published. Bondholders, if a missing budget is priced into the rate. The winners and losers inside the €10 billion are not knowable until the table exists.
- **Type:** state failure (primary balance, debt, and a budget process that has already opened one year on provisional twelfths). It is also a transfer fight about who fills the envelope. It is not, on these sources, a market-failure story with a measured price wedge.
- **Truth of problem diagnosis (0–10):** **7.5**
- **Confidence:** **Medium** that a multi-billion federal gap through 2029 is what the press, citing the Monitoring Committee, is describing, and that 5.2 percent and 107.9 percent are the figures that article prints. **Weak** on whether €10 billion is the right markup over €7.7 billion, on whether “under control” means the 3 percent treaty line or something softer, and on the interest-snowball year. The article’s “today 5.2 percent” and its “end of term, 5.2 percent, if nothing is done” sit side by side. That tension is reported, not resolved.
- **Sources:** VRT NWS, byline 6 July 2026 (Anne Vanrenterghem), read in full. VRT NWS, 3 Sep 2026 (Stefan Grommen), read in full. VRT NWS, 15 Oct 2026 and 19 Oct 2026 (Ivan De Vadder), read in full. VRT NWS, 2 Nov 2026 (Jens Van Reet), read in full. HLN 17 Oct 2026 headline only.

## 2. Mechanism

Named mechanism(s):

1. **Path versus year.** A deficit that the committee places at the end of the term is not closed by one budget. The instrument is an effort **by 2029** of €10 billion, which VRT on 15 October explicitly contrasts with €4 billion for 2027 alone.
2. **Floor plus slippage.** €7.7 billion is the reported committee floor. €10 billion is the political aim above it. The mechanism, if it is one, is to aim high so that dropped lines still leave a real consolidation.
3. **Expenditure versus revenue.** De Wever on 3 September says consolidation so far was on the expenditure side, that a compromise will contain measures of every kind, and that the revenue tap will not be wide open. He also says the tax burden should be lower in 2029 than at the start of the term, once the tax reform is at cruising speed. The HLN headline calls the same effort “besparingen.” Mahdi, quoted on 19 October, says the €10 billion can be found without VAT.
4. **Commitment under veto.** Putting the full number back on the table, after partners killed a fallback they called an index attack, is supposed to force a five-party deal on the real horizon. Last year’s search is the hopeful precedent: it looked failed, then a package reported as €9.2 billion was agreed on 24 November 2025.
5. **Market and European calendar.** A voted path is supposed to matter for the expenditure norm and for the rate at which the state rolls debt. No spread, auction, or Commission euro requirement was read for this envelope.

Does the instrument actually attack that mechanism?

Mechanism 1 is the part that fits. A multi-year envelope matches a multi-year gap better than the 2027 button the kern had just dropped. That is why this row is not a clone of the fallback. Mechanism 2 would fit if the extra €2.3 billion over the committee floor were a published slippage margin. It is a round political number. Nothing read here shows the bridge from €7.7 billion to €10 billion.

Mechanism 3 does not yet fire. Three public descriptions of the same euros cannot all be the instrument: expenditure savings (the HLN word), a mix with the revenue tap mostly shut and the tax burden falling (the September interview), and “€10 billion without VAT” (Mahdi). Until a table splits expenditure and revenue, the envelope does not choose a mechanism. It chooses a bargaining total. The VAT sketch that would have been a priced tax shift is a different proposal and, on the 19 October account, a flat rise was politically off the table that weekend.

Mechanism 4 is a negotiating tactic, not a budget. VRT on 15 October already drew the lesson of the detour: red lines pay. A return to €10 billion that leaves those red lines standing pushes the residual onto whoever has the weaker veto. The 2 November failure is the mechanism meeting the vetoes. The prime minister offered the resignation because the partners still did not agree where the €10 billion comes from. VRT’s account that day is a left-right split: Vooruit, Les Engagés and CD&V want more from the “strongest shoulders”; MR complains there are too many taxes. A target that cannot say who pays does not consolidate anything.

Mechanism 5 is untested in these sources. A communiqué that talks will resume is not a signal that binds a debt agency or a rating committee. The July article is clear that 15 October is the filing date the government itself treats as real. By 2 November there was still no agreement, and VRT notes that even a sudden deal would be hard to get through parliament in time. That is a calendar fact about the process. It is not a measured effect on the spread.

**mechanism_fit (0–10):** **4.0**

## 5. Distribution & transfer constraint

- Winners: the federal treasury, and anyone who would otherwise pay a larger interest bill, if the €10 billion becomes new cash against the no-policy path. Parties whose red lines survive the eventual mix. That political win is not a euro.
- Losers: unnamed, which is the distributional fact. The September interview points at expenditure and at a tax burden that does not rise. The 2 November deadlock points at health care, at “the strongest shoulders,” and at taxes MR says are already too present. People cannot be told which of those is the bill.
- Exit / entry risk for productive base: a consolidation that slows debt service can leave more room for a lower tax on work, which is the September steelman. A consolidation that raises labour taxes, or that cuts health capacity people then buy privately, does the opposite. Neither leg is in the 17 October decision. Firms and workers cannot yet see a price.
- New permanent clientele? No new subsidy and no new agency. The capture path is the open table. VRT’s 15 October account already says red lines pay. Each protected clientele (health growth, the index, a VAT veto, a demand that a slice come from large wealth) shrinks the set of lines that can actually move.

**capture_risk (0–10):** **6.0**

## 9. Recommendation

`amend`

Keep the problem and drop the pretence that the search is the policy. The minimum fix is a public bridge, before any leaderboard treats €10 billion as cash: the Monitoring Committee no-policy path, the yearly euros from 2027 through 2029, a split between expenditure and revenue, and a column that keeps measures already decided in 2025 out of this envelope. Vote that table, or say plainly that 2027 opens on twelfths.

Option B, abolishing the aim and running the no-policy path, is the honest alternative if the coalition will not name the lines. It is a worse budget than a voted table, and it is a better budget than another month of a round number. The 2027 fallback stays rejected on its own row. The VAT sketch stays on its own row.
