## Steelman (proponent’s best case)

Flanders has to land a budget balance in 2027, and the September exercise already forced sharp choices on every minister and every entity. De Lijn is not exempt. The company is not in financial balance. The government lists three causes it calls temporary: the fare-evasion target is not fully met, electric buses are arriving late so the older diesel fleet stays in service longer, and future income from basic accessibility and fare autonomy is not here yet. Running new kilometres on top of that gap would make the hole larger.

The decision, as Van Achter stated it for the mobility minister on 7 October 2026, is therefore not “cut the organisation”. The Flemish budget still shows a plus: €25 million extra for De Lijn in 2027, with a further growth path in view. That €25 million is invested in the organisation so the accounts can be repaired. After that, the same government says, De Lijn can grow again in offer, capacity and quality. The alternative that was refused is the one riders actually fear: scrap more rides, or raise fares again. Local councils are disappointed that 2027 will not bring extra offer. The government says it shares the ambition to go faster and that, this year, the ambition loses to the balance.

On the government’s own long comparison, De Lijn is not a starved shell. Van Achter put 2015 funding at €994.7 million and 2026 funding at €1,390.5 million (+39.8 percent), and at €1,470.5 million (+48 percent) if one fifth of the €400 million green-bus order is counted in. She put inflation over that stretch near 39 percent, and concluded that the full picture still rises. A quarter cut in “operating means”, the figure the director-general and the opposition use, leaves out investment and, in her telling, mis-states the effort. Pupil transport, in the same answer, gets €14 million on top of an initial €139 million, which she offers as proof that the most vulnerable riders were protected while the network itself was not asked for another round of scrapped rides.

Coalition partner Vooruit, through Els Robeyns, adds the political bargain: they fought for €125 million extra to strengthen De Lijn, there is no saving on the offer, and growth returns from 2028. N-VA floor speaker Maertens says the €25 million is there to get the budget structurally in order, and that pushing the problem onto the next generation is the worse choice. On the steelman, a year of repair without a new fare hike and without a new wave of cancelled rides is what a consolidation looks like when the alternative is to pretend the expansion line can buy frequency the company cannot pay for.

## 1. Problem claim

- **Metric / baseline:** Two different baselines are in the same plenary, and they are not the same claim.
  - **Coalition expansion line.** Opposition (Sarah T'Joens, Vlaams Belang) says that from 2027 only half of the original €50 million extra remains, and that later years are an open question. The minister’s answer does not offer a different 2027 plus. It says the plus is €25 million. Half of €50 million is the arithmetic the press (VRT 23 October, Nieuwsblad 29 September) also uses. The €50 million / €125 million cruise-speed path is the line ministers have described in earlier debates as De Lijn **and** vervoer op maat together. This memo does not treat a retrieved budget-PDF snippet as a page-checked table.
  - **What the €25 million buys.** Van Achter: local governments will see **no extra offer in 2027**. The €25 million is used to restore financial balance. Causes named: fare-evasion target missed, delayed electric buses, diesel fleet kept longer, income from basic accessibility and fare autonomy still ahead.
  - **Contested long baseline.** Ann Schoubs (De Lijn), quoted by the opposition from *De Standaard*, says operating resources fell by about a quarter over fifteen years. Van Achter answers with the €994.7 million (2015) to €1,390.5 million (2026) total, plus the bus order. Those totals were **not** re-added from a budget table for this memo. Bogdan Vanden Berghe (Groen) says the 2027 plan still books €30 million from fare evasion and €40 million from ticket sales, and that the Court of Audit already called those targets unrealistic. That Court of Audit text was **not** opened.
  - **Service already removed, earlier than this decision.** VRT (23 October 2026) says earlier savings scrapped 3,200 stops and dozens of lines. Vanden Berghe says “thousands” of stops and many cancelled rides. The 3,200 figure is press. It belongs to the earlier operating cut, not to this €25 million assignment.
- **Who is hurt:** Riders who already lost a stop or a line, and who were told the expansion line would bring frequency or fill a blind spot, get no extra offer in 2027. People without a car (the campaign names older people, and the poverty network) feel that first. Taxpayers are hurt if a “temporary” gap is plugged with the growth euro and then reappears. De Lijn staff are hurt if the next repair is again a timetable cut. Local councils and the fifteen transport regions planned on an offer that the minister’s answer now says will not come in 2027.
- **Type:** state failure (operator accounts, and a Flemish balance that has to be reached in 2027) mixed with a public-service problem (a network that was already thinned). It is also a transfer fight over whether the expansion euro is for kilometres or for the hole.
- **Truth of problem diagnosis (0–10):** **6.0**
- **Confidence:** **Strong** that the government has decided there is no extra De Lijn offer in 2027 and that the €25 million plus is for the operating balance. **Medium** that the plus is half of a €50 million expansion line still on the coalition path. **Weak** on the fifteen-year quarter, on the Court of Audit sentence, and on any multi-year halving beyond 2027.
- **Sources:** Flemish Parliament plenary 7 October 2026; VRT NWS 7 October 2026 and 23 October 2026; VVSG 22 October 2026 (association table, not a budget article).

The diagnosis that De Lijn’s 2027 accounts need repair, and that another wave of scrapped rides or a fare hike is a bad way to repair them, is credible. The diagnosis that this is “not a saving” and that growth is simply waiting its turn is only half true: against the expansion line the government itself had been defending, 2027 is a smaller plus, and that plus buys no rides.

## 2. Mechanism

Named mechanism(s):

1. **One-year balance plug.** A €25 million exploitation plus in 2027 is paid into De Lijn and assigned to the operating gap (fare-evasion miss, delayed electrification, diesel kept in service), not to new kilometres.
2. **Refused alternatives.** The government says the other options on the table were scrapping rides or raising fares, and that both were rejected for 2027.
3. **Deferred growth.** A further growth path stays “in view”. Robeyns says there is no saving on the offer and that growth returns from 2028. No trigger, occupancy test, or euro amount for 2028 is in the answer that was read out.
4. **Separate protection for a named group.** Pupil transport +€14 million on a stated €139 million base, in the same answer. That is a different envelope. It is not evidence that the ordinary network grows.

Does the instrument actually attack that mechanism?

Mechanism (1) attacks a cash gap if, and only if, the gap is about €25 million and is temporary. The plenary does not publish the size of the gap. If the fare-evasion and ticket targets stay in the plan at the levels Vanden Berghe names, the plug recreates itself next year. Mechanism (2) is real for 2027: the stated choice is “no new ride cuts, no new fare hike”, paid for by not buying the extra offer. That is a coherent one-year choice. It does not undo the stops and lines already scrapped. Mechanism (3) does not attack anything yet. A growth path “in view”, after the 2026 half of the expansion line was already put on hold and the 2027 half has now followed, is a sentence, not a rule. Mechanism (4) can be true and still leave the commuter and the rural rider with a flat network.

The coalition’s older mobility mechanism — more people leave the car because the bus is more frequent, including outside the cities — is not what this euro does. Van Achter says the ambition is shared and that 2027 will not deliver extra offer.

**mechanism_fit (0–10):** **5.0**

## 3. Options considered

| ID | Option | One-line effect |
|----|--------|-----------------|
| A | Status quo | Keep the coalition expansion line at +€50 million in 2027 and spend it on frequency and measured blind spots, and close the operating gap with a separate, published decision. |
| B | Abolish / deregulate | Abolish the rule that the expansion euro may silently become deficit finance. Publish an occupancy floor, drop the obligation to run near-empty trips the regions no longer want to buy, and let a transport region procure that service from De Lijn or another operator with a fixed euro per useful passenger-km. |
| C | Price / tax reform | Pay for the gap with prices that match the cause: collect the fare-evasion euro that is already the target, and price scarce road and parking space where the bus is the alternative, instead of moving the expansion line into the hole. Keep a social tariff. |
| D | Conditional transfer | Pay the €25 million only against a published 2027 gap. Any euro not needed for that defined gap buys frequency or a named blind-spot service in the same year. Retire own-revenue targets the Court of Audit has actually called unrealistic, once that text is on the table. |
| E | Public provision / invest | Restore the full +€50 million for service and take the Flemish 2027 consolidation from a programme with a weaker mobility return. This is the protesters’ ask (“Hier trekken wij de lijn”). |
| F | Pilot | One transport region, one year: spend a stated slice of the €25 million on frequency where occupancy is already high, publish cost per new passenger-km, and scale only if that beats filling the gap. |
| G | **This proposal** | 2027: +€25 million instead of the €50 million expansion line, spent on financial balance, no extra offer, no new fare hike and no new ride cuts, growth path left unspecified, pupil-transport top-up kept separate. |

A is what the expansion line was for. B is the deregulatory option: stop hiding a network decision inside a deficit line. G does the cash repair and leaves the service promise for later, without a test that would make “later” real.

## 5. Distribution & transfer constraint

- Winners: The Flemish 2027 balance, if the €25 million really closes a one-off gap and is not spent twice in the political story. De Lijn’s accounts, in the narrow sense that a hole is filled without another timetable cut this year. Pupil-transport users, if the separate +€14 million is real. Taxpayers, to the extent the unspent half of the €50 million line is money not raised or not borrowed.
- Losers: Riders who needed the expansion line for frequency or a restored stop. Transport regions and municipalities that had been preparing offer on the larger euro (the government says it understands that disappointment). The modal-shift claim, for one more year. People whose alternative is a car they cannot drive or cannot afford: the campaign’s older-rider and poverty-network point is the right population, even though 169 stories are not a baseline.
- Exit / entry risk for productive base: A flat, already thinned bus network raises the cost of reaching a job without a car and pushes marginal trips back into cars. That is an entry cost for workers and a congestion cost for everyone else. It is not a factory-exit story. De Lijn itself does not gain a new permanent subsidy clientele beyond the company that already exists; it does gain the habit of absorbing the growth euro into the P&L.
- New permanent clientele? No new agency. The risk is the opposite of a new grant line: the incumbent operator captures the expansion euro as deficit finance, and the regions learn that “growth” can be postponed again at the next September exercise.

**capture_risk (0–10):** **5.5**

## 7. Belgian competence & implementation

- Competent level: Flanders. De Lijn is the Flemish public-transport company. The exploitation envelope and the budget balance are Flemish. Transport regions shape the offer but do not vote the Flemish budget.
- EU constraint: No EU rule forces this haircut or forbids it. Public-service obligations and a modal-shift target sit in the background. They do not pick the 2027 euro.
- Admin capacity / dual-structure friction: Fifteen transport regions were preparing an expansion that 2027 will not deliver. The operator and the minister already disagree on which denominator is “the” De Lijn budget. The minister was not in the chamber on 7 October; a colleague read the answer. That is friction, not a reason to move the file to another level of government. Basic accessibility was built as a budget-neutral redesign and then given a growth path; using the growth path to refill the operating budget collapses those two logics into one line.

**competence_fit (0–10):** **7.5**

## 9. Recommendation

`amend`

Minimum fix, without which the label should be dropped:

1. Publish the 2027 gap the €25 million is meant to close, split by the causes the government already names (fare evasion, e-bus delay and diesel, basic-accessibility income).
2. Put the Court of Audit view on the fare-evasion and ticket targets next to those targets. Drop or replace any target that paper has already called unrealistic.
3. Write the clawback: any part of the €25 million not needed for that defined gap buys frequency or a named blind-spot service in 2027. Do not book a euro that buys no rides as expansion.
4. Keep the pupil-transport top-up on its own line so it cannot be cited as proof that the ordinary network grew.
5. Either restore a dated 2028 euro for kilometres, or say in the budget text that the cruise-speed path has moved. “In view” is not a path.
