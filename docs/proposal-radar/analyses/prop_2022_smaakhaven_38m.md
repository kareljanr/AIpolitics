## Executive judgment

Smaakhaven packages three real things (heritage reuse of Loodswezen, tourism marketing of Flanders food, B2B networking for horeca/F&B) into one **prestige flagship** with a vocabulary of *ecosystem*, *innovation*, *talent*, *living database*, and *social fabric*—almost none of which, as stated on the official ambition page, is operationalised as a **measurable theory of change with ROI**.

The food sector in Flanders is economically material. That fact **does not** imply that a public-funded culinary experience centre with opening target **2030** is the highest-marginal-value use of **€38 million** of relance cash. On the published ambition text, the project fails a basic public-investment test: **problem → mechanism → metric → counterfactual → cost**.

## Steelman (best case, written in good faith)

### What is true and useful in the proponent world

1. **Flanders has a genuine food / horeca / agri-processing cluster.** Export-oriented food manufacturing, retail chains, logistics, and a dense restaurant scene are not a fantasy. A “brand story” for food tourism is a legitimate soft-power and B2C marketing goal for a tourism agency. **Medium** confidence that *some* public marketing of food can move overnight stays and product discovery.

2. **Heritage buildings need a use.** Loodswezen + Boeienloods on the Scheldekaaien are visible monuments. Leaving them empty is a real option cost (decay, opportunity of site). Adaptive reuse can bundle conservation with activity. A pure **heritage conservation + flexible commercial lease** story is stronger than the full “ecosystem hub” story.

3. **Sector coordination failures exist in theory.** Farmers, processors, HORECA, schools, and R&D can under-invest in joint standards, skills pipelines, and export promotion because benefits are diffuse. A neutral platform *could* reduce search costs for partnerships (matchmaking, demo kitchens, buyer days).

4. **Demand signals from the industry are not zero.** Official timeline cites **81% positive** among surveyed stakeholders (2022 concept phase) and large outreach in 2025 (tens of thousands of entrepreneurs contacted / “acquainted” at trade fairs). That is evidence of *interest in a free or subsidised venue*, not of willingness to pay full economic cost—but it is not pure invention.

5. **Private co-investment path is at least gestured at.** The site pushes “founding partners” and later commercial partnerships. If private capital and operating risk were majority and public money were only residual heritage gap-funding, the steelman improves dramatically.

6. **Long lead time can be used well.** Opening target **2030** allows design quality, co-creation, and a proper business plan *if* governance forces hard gates (kill/scale) before renovation cash is fully sunk.

### Best-case success definition (what would have to happen)

For the project to clear a truth-seeking bar *ex post*:

| Metric (illustrative, not claimed by them) | Why it matters |
|--------------------------------------------|----------------|
| Net **additional** tourist nights / F&B spend in BE attributable to Smaakhaven | Not reallocated restaurant visits from elsewhere in Antwerp |
| **Private** opex coverage ≥ 70–100% within 3 years of opening | Ends permanent soft budget |
| Documented B2B deals / export contracts with additionality | “Network” becomes cash, not LinkedIn |
| Training graduates with placement in shortage roles | Academy pillar ≠ event catering |
| Cost per additional job / € export below alternative instruments (export vouchers, skills, R&D tax) | Opportunity cost |

If those were published *ex ante* with independent review, clownpoints would fall even if we still preferred a cheaper instrument.

## 1. Problem claim — what market failure is this solving?

### Stated problem (reconstructed from ambition language)

From [smaakhaven.be/nl/ambitie](https://www.smaakhaven.be/nl/ambitie) (accessed 2026-07-27):

- Put the **full food chain** “from farmer to plate” in the spotlight  
- **Support every link** and bring them closer  
- Create links between food and the creative industry  
- Address **“kwetsbaarheid van de voedingssector”** via talent, lifelong learning, and initiatives to make the sector **economically stronger**  
- Connect big firms & startups, education & firms, research & producers, producers & consumers  
- Make Flanders proud; visitors “experience” food culture; strengthen **economic and social fabric**

### Problem audit

| Claimed problem | Is it real? | Grade | Does a €38m centre uniquely fix it? |
|-----------------|-------------|-------|-------------------------------------|
| Low visibility of Flanders food abroad | Partially — brand competition is real | Medium | No; destination marketing, trade fairs, content already exist |
| Fragmented chain / weak cooperation | Partially — coordination costs exist | Weak–Medium | No; platforms, FEVIA, VLAM, cluster orgs, digital B2B |
| Skills / talent shortage in HORECA | Real in many EU markets | Medium | Weak link; vocational schools + wage/conditions dominate |
| Need for “innovation” in food | Buzzword; real R&D sits in firms/labs | Weak as stated | Demo kitchens ≠ R&D productivity |
| Social fabric / stories / emotions around food | Preference / culture, not market failure | Speculative as public investment case | No public-euro test |
| Empty / underused heritage building | Real local planning issue | Medium | Yes *for adaptive reuse*; does not require culinary-ecosystem ideology |

**truth_problem = 2.5** remains: the *binding* constraints on Flanders food competitiveness (labour cost/wedge, energy, permitting, retail buyer power, global commodity prices, HORECA margins) are almost **absent** from the ambition page. The page optimises **narrative completeness**, not **diagnosed scarcity**.

## 2. Ambition page — claim-by-claim fact check

Official structure: **Promoten · Ondersteunen · Verbinden**, then pillars **Academy · Entertainment · Business**.

### 2.1 “Promoten” — Flanders as full ecosystem master + innovator

| Ambition text | Literal meaning | Evidence on site | Truth-seeking read |
|---------------|-----------------|------------------|--------------------|
| Promote Flanders as region that “beheerst én vernieuwt” a full ecosystem | Nation-branding + innovation claim | No baseline metrics (exports, Michelin density trend, visitor food spend) | Marketing slogan. **Strong** as aspiration; **Weak** as causal plan |
| Show traditions, craft, innovation physically + events + online platform | Omnichannel content | Physical centre not open until ~2030; online not specified | Until open, this is a **website + trade-fair stand** funded by a building project |
| Make Flemings proud → citizen ambassadors | Soft PR | Unmeasurable | Not a falsifiable public objective |

**Verdict:** Promotion can be done at a fraction of €38m (content, fairs, VLAM-style campaigns). Building a permanent “showroom of pride” is a **luxury complement**, not the least-cost instrument.

### 2.2 “Ondersteunen” — fix sector vulnerability via talent & lifelong learning

| Ambition text | What’s missing for ROI |
|---------------|------------------------|
| Talent development, lifelong learning | No seats/year, no cost per trainee, no placement KPI, no link to existing Syntra/HORECA schools |
| “Concrete initiatives” to make sector economically stronger | No list of instruments with € tags beyond the building itself |

**Verdict:** If the binding constraint is skills, **buy training outcomes** (vouchers, apprenticeships, wage top-ups for shortage roles)—do not route skills policy through a tourism monument. Mechanism fit is **low**.

### 2.3 “Verbinden” — connect every node in the chain

Connecting producers, chefs, researchers, startups, and consumers is the classic **cluster / platform** claim.

Economic logic that *can* work:

- High search costs + high trust needs → co-location or repeated interaction raises match rate.

What the ambition page actually offers:

- “Inspiring experimental space”  
- “Stories, traditions, emotions”  
- “Living database” (Business pillar)  
- Product testing for firms; workshops for visitors  

**Missing:**

- Why existing venues (Flanders Expo, food fairs, university pilot plants, private test kitchens, retail innovation labs) fail  
- Price of access for SMEs vs multinationals (capture risk)  
- Digital-first alternative cost  

**Verdict:** “Verbinden” without a **priced matchmaking product** and **additionality study** is NGO-speak. A living database is a **software project**, not a Scheldt-side renovation.

### 2.4 Three pillars — Academy / Entertainment / Business

| Pillar | Stated content | Public-goods intensity | Substitution |
|--------|----------------|------------------------|--------------|
| **Academy** | Lifelong learning, research, future growth, talent | Medium if real courses with credentials | Schools, Syntra, universities, private academies |
| **Entertainment** | International aura, stories, experience, pride, coherence | Low (private experience goods) | Restaurants, festivals, museums (MAS, etc.), food tours |
| **Business** | Collaboration, meetings, structure, living database | Medium if pure B2B platform | Chambers, FEVIA, trade fairs, LinkedIn, private hubs |

The **Entertainment** pillar is the commercial core of a visitor attraction. That is fine for a **private** operator maximising ticket + F&B margin. It is a weak justification for **relance capital** aimed (in theory) at recovery productivity.

Peter Goossens’ quote (vaandeldrager) supplies **celebrity endorsement**, not evidence.

### 2.5 Timeline realism (official tijdlijn)

| Year | Claim | Implication |
|------|-------|-------------|
| 2021 | Project start; market research of culinary centres | Peer-copying is fine; does not prove BE-specific ROI |
| 2022 | Concept; **81% stakeholder support** | Selection bias: ask culinary stakeholders if they want a subsidised centre |
| 2023–24 | Building studies, participation, concept functions | Process legitimacy ≠ economic case |
| 2025 | Design team HUB + Origin; heavy fair presence | Marketing spend before ops |
| 2026 | Focus on **exploitation** model | Correct priority—should have gated capital on this |
| 2027 | Renovation starts (planned) | Cash intensity rises |
| **2030** | Opening (if on plan) | **~8–9 years** from announcement to doors |

**Capital productivity problem:** €38m (order of magnitude committed from 2022) sits in a project that does not generate operating surplus until ~2030. Even ignoring inflation and scope creep, social discounting hurts hard.

Illustrative opportunity cost (order-of-magnitude, **Speculative** arithmetic for intuition—not an audit):

- €38m × 4% real social discount rate ≈ **€1.5m per year** forever as forgone alternative yield  
- Over 8 years pre-opening: roughly **€10–12m** of pure waiting cost in present-value terms before a single ticket  
- Alternative: ~hundreds of deep energy retrofits, or a material cut in a distortionary tax expenditure, or capitalised vocational training places  

**Fiscal honesty on the €38m figure itself is relatively high** (decision trail exists). **Honesty on benefits is near zero** on the public ambition page.

### 2.6 What the ambition page does *not* contain (decisive)

Checked on official ambition / home / timeline / building pages (2026-07-27):

- [ ] Visitor projections (base / high / low)  
- [ ] Ticket price assumptions  
- [ ] Operating budget / subsidy-to-operate after opening  
- [ ] Jobs (FTE construction vs permanent; gross vs net)  
- [ ] Attribution method for tourism spend  
- [ ] Private vs public capital share at steady state  
- [ ] Kill criteria if partners do not fund opex  
- [ ] Comparison to cheaper instruments  
- [ ] Independent CBA or Rekenhof-style pre-audit linked from site  

Absence of these is not a minor communications gap. For a **€38m** project it is the difference between **policy** and **brand theatre**.

### 6.1 Known public money

| Item | Amount / term | Confidence | Source class |
|------|----------------|------------|--------------|

### 6.2 ROI framework (what rigorous analysis requires)

Define:

\[
ROI_{social} = \frac{PV(\text{additional consumer surplus + producer surplus + net tax})}{PV(\text{public cost})}
\]

For tourism projects, literature routinely finds:

- **Gross** visitor spending ≫ **net additional** spending (crowding out, locals, multi-purpose trips)  
- Construction jobs are **temporary** and mostly **reallocated** from other sites  
- Brand effects decay without continuous marketing spend  

Without a published CBA, the **default Bayesian prior** for a mid-sized European “thematic experience centre” is: **social ROI < 1**, especially when opening is a decade after announcement and the concept is multi-mandate (academy + fun + B2B + heritage + pride).

### 6.3 Back-of-envelope break-even (honesty check)

Suppose post-2030 the centre needs to “justify” €38m public capital at 4% real:

- Required **perpetual** social surplus ≈ **€1.5m/year** net of private profits already counted  
- Or amortised over 25 years ≈ **€2.3m/year** average net social benefit  

That is a lot of *additional* value after:

- subtracting displaced restaurant meals elsewhere in Antwerp  
- subtracting what VLAM / private brands would have spent on marketing anyway  
- subtracting deadweight of taxes that funded the €38m  

Ticket economics alone: at €15 net margin per visit, you need **100,000+ truly additional paying visits per year** forever just to approach capital service—**before** opex subsidy. The ambition page does not even attempt this arithmetic.

**fiscal_honesty = 6.5:** the *input* euro figure is clear; the *output* story is not. Score is not 2 because they did not fabricate a fake “€200m impact” press number on the ambition page—they simply **omitted** the hard numbers. Omission is still a form of fiscal un-seriousness for public capital.

## 10. Recommendation

**`reject`** further public top-ups and any opex guarantee.

Minimum amend path if political sunk-cost locks construction:

1. **Hard opex rule:** no operating subsidy; private SPV takes demand risk.  
2. **Publish CBA** with additionality and kill criteria before renovation main phase (2027).  
3. **Split the product:** heritage shell vs commercial F&B vs training—train with vouchers, don’t bury training in monument opex.  
4. **Sunset partner privileges** that recreate semi-public clienteles.

Default abundance-maximising alternative: **B + D** — commercial adaptive reuse of Loodswezen + portable food-export/skills instruments.

---

## 11. Falsifier

> We reverse toward **amend/support** if, before main renovation spend locks, an independent CBA (Rekenhof-grade or equivalent) shows **PV(net additional benefits) > PV(public costs)** under conservative additionality, **and** private partners commit binding opex coverage ≥ 70% for the first 5 operating years, **and** Academy seats have placement KPIs competitive with existing vocational providers.

Absent that, clown 8 stands.

## 14. Bottom line (plain language)

The [ambition page](https://www.smaakhaven.be/nl/ambitie) is fluent **tourism-agency prose**: promote, support, connect; academy, entertainment, business; pride, stories, innovation, living database. Read as literature, it is coherent. Read as an **investment memo**, it is almost empty.

**Fancy words are not neutral.** They perform a political function: they make a landmark renovation feel like industrial policy. Honest analysis separates:

- **Heritage reuse** (partially defensible),  
- **Food marketing** (cheaper tools exist),  
- **Skills** (wrong instrument),  
- **B2B networking** (wrong price point as public monument),  
- **Emotions and ambassadorship** (not a budget category).

Until ROI is published and risk is private, Smaakhaven remains a high-clownpoints public project: not because food is silly, but because **€38m of other people’s money is being asked to underwrite a story**.
