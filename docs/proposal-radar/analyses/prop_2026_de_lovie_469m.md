## Steelman (proponent’s best case)

Flanders protects a large stock of buildings and cannot finish every restoration at once. In December 2025 the Flemish government opened one call for this legislature, under the motto “Fier op dat van hier”, with a total budget of €25 million. The call is for large or long works on protected heritage or heritage landscapes, a maximum execution term of five years, and a public opening. Eligible cost for the premium calculation sits between €5 million and €7.5 million excluding VAT. The premium itself is capped at €6 million and uses the percentages already in the Immovable Heritage decision. A jury ranks the dossiers. The government was to decide by 1 October 2026 and then sign a multi-year premium agreement. Own money is not a footnote: proportionality, including what the initiator adds on top of the subsidised part, is 20 percent of the score.

Nineteen dossiers came in. The minister’s stated choice was to back five strong sites instead of spreading the envelope until nobody finishes. Kasteel De Lovie in Poperinge is one of the five, at €4.69 million. The building is a protected castle of the mid-nineteenth century, built as a summer house for the Van Merris family, used in the First World War as headquarters of the British Fifth Army during the Battle of Passchendaele, later a provincial tuberculosis sanatorium, and now owned by the care organisation that has worked on the domain for decades. The stones were already failing. At the end of 2025 the facade phase started: rusted anchors between the natural-stone facing and the brick, loose stones, a two-year repair of anchors, stone and the monumental stairs. That phase was priced at €3.5 million, of which €2 million from the Heritage Agency and the rest from vzw Domein De Lovie. The full restoration was then estimated at €12.6 million.

The new premium is the interior slice. The castle has been in scaffolding for about a year. With this cheque the organisation says it can largely complete the restoration: cellar, ground floor, first floor and roof, while the care budgets stay untouched and a separate castle financing stream continues to look for private money. The minister’s account of the same cheque, as carried by the regional press, is restoration of the interior, the roof and the exterior joinery, plus better access, fire safety and energy performance. People the organisation supports already bake, cook and pack gifts for visitors in the dining room, the tea room and the shop. The ambition is to carry that contact through the whole castle, up to a fully accessible lookout of the kind the roof was in 1917. On this reading the public euro buys a finite capital repair of a protected building that is already open to visitors, leverages an owner contribution, and does it through a capped contest rather than an annual operating subsidy.

## 1. Problem claim

- **Metric / baseline:** Two different claims sit on one cheque. The fabric claim has a physical baseline: facade stones coming loose, rusted metal anchors, a phase-1 contract already on site (Focus-WTV, 11 Dec 2025). The full restoration was then estimated at €12.6 million. No public condition survey for the interior was retrieved this session, and no visitor count, energy figure or signed match euro for the 2026 award was retrieved. The inclusion claim is qualitative: “het meest inclusieve kasteel van het land” (VRT, quoting the organisation) and, in a later line the same day, “het meest inclusieve kasteel van Vlaanderen” (Focus-WTV, quoting project coordinator Katrien Goudeseune). The call’s own problem statement is identity and public opening (“Fier op dat van hier”), plus large works on protected goods. Minister Weyts, in the Nieuwsblad/GVA account of the Diest announcement, put the protected stock at 11,000 buildings. That stock figure is his round number in the press, not an inventory table checked here.
- **Who is hurt:** Future users of a protected building if the shell fails; the Westhoek public if a war-history site stays closed behind scaffolding; the people De Lovie supports if the only workplace that mixes them with visitors is a half-finished castle; Flemish taxpayers if €4.69 million is assigned by a beauty contest while ordinary heritage files wait. The care mission itself is outside this cheque: Goudeseune says the care budgets are not the source.
- **Type:** market failure / public good for the fabric of a protected monument (diffuse option value, owner cannot capture the heritage spillover), plus a preference for an inclusive visitor programme, plus a transfer fight inside a heritage budget the same press cycle describes as being cut.
- **Truth of problem diagnosis (0–10):** **6.0**
- **Confidence:** **Medium** that the facade was actively failing in December 2025 and that phase 1 is €3.5 million with €2 million from the agency (one regional broadcaster, on the day the minister started the works; same euros repeated in a later copy). **Medium** that a Flemish premium of €4.69 million for De Lovie was announced on 30 Oct 2026 as part of a five-site list totalling about €24 million (VRT body text and VRT package piece agree on 4.69; Focus rounds the same award to 4.7). **Weak** on the inclusion and visitor-economy diagnosis: no baseline, and the roof lookout is still described as a dream.
- **Sources:** VRT NWS 30 Oct 2026 (unit article and the five-monument piece); Focus-WTV 30 Oct 2026; Focus-WTV 11 Dec 2025; VR bijlage 12 Dec 2025; Nieuwsblad/GVA 30 Oct 2026 as indexed text (full page not opened).

The decaying protected shell is a real problem. The further claim that this premium is what makes De Lovie the most inclusive castle in the country, or that a roof lookout is part of the repair, is a mission statement.

## 2. Mechanism

Named mechanism(s):

1. **Capped competitive capital premium.** One legislature call, €25 million, jury rank, multi-year agreement, premium cap €6 million, eligible-cost window €5–7.5 million excluding VAT, execution at most five years. Only works necessary to keep or revalue heritage characteristics are subsidizable. Deeldossiers follow the framework agreement.
2. **Match.** The owner’s money on top of the subsidised part is 20 percent of the jury score. A financial plan is a required dossier piece.
3. **Public opening.** The project must open the protected good, or at least a representative part, including during the works. That is an entry condition, and “ontsluiting” plus the motto is 15 percent of the score.
4. **Concentration.** Weyts: 19 dossiers, five chosen, so the money is not splintered. De Lovie is the West-Flemish care-campus site on a list whose other names are the Belfort, the Leuven town hall, the Diest citadel and the Rubenshuis.
5. **Reuse by the owner.** The beneficiary wants the restored rooms to carry horeca, meetings, lodging and events inside the existing care operation, and still dreams of an accessible roof lookout.

Does the instrument actually attack that mechanism?

For (1) and (2), a restoration premium tied to a management plan is the instrument Flemish law already uses for protected fabric. The December 2025 annex says the planned works must sit in an approved beheersplan, and the premium percentages are those in articles 11.2.9 to 11.2.12 of the Immovable Heritage decision. This session did not re-read those percentages and does not state a rate. The €4.69 million figure is below the €6 million cap, so it is arithmetically possible as a premium. It is not labelled including or excluding VAT in the press.

For (3), opening a building the organisation already partly opens is a weak marginal test. The tea room exists. The premium does not, on the public record, buy a measured increase in access.

For (4), concentration finishes five projects and drops fourteen. That is a real allocation rule. It is also a prestige filter. The €5 million floor means a modest repair cannot enter this call at all. Projects may cost more than €7.5 million, but the premium base stops at that ceiling. The design pushes applicants toward a large eligible slice.

For (5), the fit breaks. Goudeseune (Focus-WTV, 30 Oct 2026) says the money lets them realise the dreams: tourist welcome, restaurant, lodging, B2B events, with the people they support. The same interview still calls the roof lookout a dream. VRT’s package piece lists “ruimte voor horeca, vergaderingen, evenementen en bezoeken” as where the €4.69 million goes. The call text subsidises heritage works, not a commercial fit-out. A restored room can host a tea service. A new lodging floor or a new lookout is a different product. The public announcement does not split those euros.

The phase-1 cheque and this cheque are easy to add and easy to misuse. €2 million (Dec 2025 press) plus €4.69 million (Oct 2026 press) is €6.69 million of public money named against a €12.6 million estimate. That sum is arithmetic on two press articles. It is not an official cumulative award, and the €12.6 million figure is a year old. Against that old estimate, €4.69 million covers about half of the €9.1 million that remained after a €3.5 million first phase. “Grotendeels voltooien” (VRT, Goudeseune) is a stronger verb than that remainder. She also says private money is still being sought, and that the care budgets stay out of it. Both sentences can be true. They cannot both mean the restoration is funded.

**mechanism_fit (0–10):** **5.0**

## 3. Options considered

| ID | Option | One-line effect |
|----|--------|-----------------|
| A | Status quo | Leave the facade phase (€3.5 million, of which €2 million already public) to finish, and leave the interior on the owner’s separate castle fund and on the ordinary premium track. |
| B | Abolish / deregulate | Award no iconic-call premium to De Lovie. The owner keeps the building, the care mission and the right to run a tea room, and finances further fabric works from gifts, own reserves and the standard premium if the dossier qualifies. |
| C | Price / tax reform | Charge the users who benefit: paid visits, event hire and lodging set to cover the capital service of the non-heritage extras, with the protected shell still eligible for the ordinary premium percentage. |
| D | Conditional transfer | Sign the €4.69 million only against beheersplan lines for fabric, fire safety, access and energy; pay tranches after the non-care match is in; claw back amounts spent on a lookout, lodging or horeca fit-out. |
| E | Public provision / invest | Have the Flemish government or the municipality take the monument and restore it as a public site. The care vzw would be a tenant, not the premienemer. That swaps a capped premium for a public owner and a permanent maintenance duty. |
| F | Pilot | One interior phase only, with a published condition survey, a visitor and workplace count before and after, and a stop if the match or the eligibility split fails. |
| G | **This proposal** | Award €4.69 million as one of five iconic-call premiums so the interior, roof and joinery can be restored and the inclusive visitor use extended, while private money is still being sought and the lookout remains a dream. |

B is the abolish option: no special cheque, no new prohibition, the owner keeps every private route. D is G with the eligibility rules of the December 2025 annex actually published against this building. A is the pre-award path, which had already started the facade with a smaller public share. E makes the public the owner of a care campus’s castle, which the steelman does not ask for. G announces the cheque and leaves the split, the match and the lookout for later.

## 5. Distribution & transfer constraint

- Winners: vzw Domein De Lovie as premienemer; visitors and the people who already work in the castle’s public rooms; the contractors of the next phase; the minister’s “five pearls” narrative. West Flanders, on the press list, receives the Belfort (€5.5 million) and De Lovie (€4.69 million), about €10.2 million of the reported €24 million.
- Losers: the fourteen unnamed applicants; ordinary premium files that cannot clear a €5 million floor; Flemish taxpayers for a one-envelope capital cost of €4.69 million. Care clients are losers only if the match quietly comes from care subsidies. The beneficiary says that will not happen. The dossier is not public.
- Exit / entry risk for productive base: Negligible for the wider economy. A castle tea room and possible lodging in Poperinge do not move the productive base. The risk is local: a public capital grant underwrites rooms that then compete with unsubsidised cafés and venues in the Westhoek.
- New permanent clientele? The legal form is a multi-year capital agreement, not a new annual subsidy and not a new agency. The same owner already received €2 million for phase 1 in 2025. A second cheque inside two years is how a clientele starts, even when each cheque is finite. The jury is half the paying agency (2 of 4). The motto is the minister’s.

**capture_risk (0–10):** **5.5**

## 7. Belgian competence & implementation

- Competent level: Immovable heritage premiums are Flemish (Onroerenderfgoeddecreet; this call is a Flemish government annex of 12 Dec 2025). The owner is a private vzw in Poperinge. The city welcomes the award (mayor Christof Dejaegher, CD&V, on Focus-WTV) and is not the premienemer in the sources read here.
- EU constraint: A premium limited to non-economic heritage fabric is the ordinary cultural case. Restaurant, lodging and event hire are economic activities. If those fit-out costs sit inside the premium, state-aid rules apply. The annex retrieved here does not discuss that split. The care subsidy is a different stream and, on the beneficiary’s word, stays separate.
- Admin capacity / dual-structure friction: The agency already runs premium agreements and deeldossiers. The call’s own decision deadline was 1 October 2026. The public announcement is 30 October 2026 at the Citadel of Diest. The besluit date was not retrieved, so the lag is an announcement fact, not proof the decision missed the deadline. Five-year execution has not started in any public schedule specific to this premium. Deadlines other newsrooms gave for the Belfort, the Leuven town hall or the Diest citadel are not used as De Lovie’s date.

**competence_fit (0–10):** **7.5**

Right level, existing legal tool, owner is the applicant the call allows. The open point is the economic-use boundary, not which parliament is competent.

## 9. Recommendation

`amend`

Keep a restoration premium only if it is published as option D. Concrete minimum fix:

- Print the besluit and the meerjarenpremieovereenkomst: eligible lines, VAT basis, premium percentage, and the owner’s signed non-care match.
- Pay against the approved beheersplan (fabric, fire safety, access, energy). A roof lookout, lodging or horeca fit-out needs its own price and its own payer.
- Release later tranches only after the private match is in. Care subsidies do not count as that match.
- Publish the jury rank for the nineteen dossiers, including the fourteen that received nothing.

Until that split exists, the award is a plausible conservation cheque with an unpriced visitor programme attached.
