## Steelman (proponent’s best case)

Closed return centres exist to hold people with no right to stay until they leave the country. That job is only real if the people who run the wings can finish a shift. ACV’s account, echoed the same week by VRT and Focus-WTV, is that verbal and physical aggression, arson and staff dropout have become ordinary, and that a growing share of residents need addiction or psychiatric support the security staff were not trained to give. Each person who drops out raises the load on the people who remain. The union’s line is that human and financial means have not moved with that caseload.

The five asks are meant to stop that spiral at the point where it is generated. A staffing guarantee in every centre, plus a ban on adding residents unless supervision is added with them, stops management from treating a bed as free capacity. A clear ceiling on complex profiles in each building stops one wing from becoming an improvised psychiatric ward inside an administrative lock-up. Higher risk, hardship and clothing allowances, frozen for years on the union’s telling, make the job recruitable again. Keeping those allowances inside the pension calculation tells a 55-year-old officer that the danger pay is part of the career, not a temporary bonus that disappears at retirement.

Kelly De Wilde’s public formulation is the clean version of the goal: without enough staff there is neither safety nor a decent service, and the authorities should take concrete measures so the centres stay both safe and humane. The minister, Anneleen Van Bossuyt, does not deny the strain. She says a backlog of years cannot be cleared in one meeting, points to extra staffing resources since she took office, and cites nearly 846 deployable staff for a maximum of 550 residents, about 1.54 per resident if every bed is full. She has also asked the buildings minister to put infrastructure works first. The union’s answer, after the 13 October cabinet meeting, is that those are medium- and long-term lines, and that the floor today is still short. A ratio on paper does not stop the incident on tonight’s wing. A cap and a short-term staff rule are how the service stays open while the new buildings, including the 50-place short-stay centre in Steenokkerzeel announced for the end of 2026, are still a construction site.

There is a return-policy steelman too. The minister’s own warning, carried by EMN, is that a strike which stops removal work leaves people in the centres, or in the country, for longer. Staff who believe they will go home in one piece are the throughput of the return system. Paying and bounding that work is cheaper than a centre that is formally open and operationally shut.

## 1. Problem claim

- **Metric / baseline:** No public incident series was found in this pass. What is on the record is qualitative and repeated. ACV (12 Oct) describes chronic understaffing, failing infrastructure in places, more incidents, and more residents with addiction or psychiatric needs than the training and roster can handle. VRT (14 Oct) quotes De Wilde: daily verbal and physical aggression, work accidents, mental-health absence, and a demand for a short-term answer on staff plus a limit on “difficult profiles”. Focus-WTV (14 Oct) reports a Brugge picket after a fire the day before at the Refuge centre, and names the six DVZ closed centres (two in Steenokkerzeel, plus Brugge, Holsbeek, Merksplas, Vottem). The minister’s counter-baseline, via EMN and the Nieuwsblad account EMN tracks, is nearly 846 deployable staff for at most 550 residents. A De Standaard interview the same day, retrieved only as an indexed excerpt, has a Brugge employee describing a shift from roughly monthly to daily incidents, including threats, arson and assault. That interview also offers a cause: people leaving prison without a residence right are being placed in the departure centre. This pass found no decision text that confirms that transfer rule.
- **Who is hurt:** Centre staff (safety, sickness, exit from the job). Residents, including people in mental-health crisis inside a security regime. The return system, if strikes or fear slow lawful removal. Neighbours of a centre when police are called to a fire or a disturbance, as Focus-WTV reports for Brugge.
- **Type:** state failure in the operation of administrative detention (placement mix, training, roster, buildings), plus a transfer fight over allowances and their pension treatment.
- **Truth of problem diagnosis (0–10):** **7**
- **Confidence:** **Medium** that staff across more than one centre are in open conflict with the service about aggression and headcount (ACV text, VRT, Focus-WTV, EMN, and a strike notice that produced pickets). **Weak** on the trend line, on “premiums unchanged for years”, on the 846/550 ratio as an audited establishment figure, and on the claim that Justice is systematically moving ex-prisoners into these centres. **Speculative** as to how much of the violence is caused by profile mix rather than by length of stay, overcrowding inside the 550, or building design.
- **Sources:** ACV Openbare Diensten 12 Oct 2026; EMN Belgium 12 Oct 2026; VRT NWS 14 Oct 2026; Focus-WTV 14 Oct 2026; De Standaard 14 Oct 2026 (indexed excerpt, page not fully read); news.belgium.be 13 Nov 2025 (existing short-stay project, context only).

## 2. Mechanism

Named mechanism(s):

1. **Capacity and roster.** Incidents and sickness shrink the effective team, which raises incidents. A floor, and a rule that resident numbers cannot rise without supervisors, attacks that feedback.
2. **Selection into the wrong regime.** Administrative detention staff are asked to hold people whose needs are clinical. A per-centre cap changes the mix. It does not create a ward, a prison place, or a treatment bed for the person who does not fit.
3. **Compensating differential.** Higher risk and clothing allowances, folded into the pension base, are a wage instrument. They can slow quits. They do not change the incident, and once pensionized they outlive the shift they were meant to price.
4. **Throughput.** The centres are a removal tool. A package that keeps staff on the wing supports throughput. A cap that excludes the people the minister says are the priority (criminal and nuisance cases without residence rights) can cut throughput of the hardest files, which is the opposite of a shorter stay.

Does the instrument actually attack that mechanism?

Partly. Points 1 and 2 of the ACV list (enough staff everywhere; no quota rise without supervision) match the roster mechanism and are the part worth keeping. Point 3 (a ceiling on complex profiles) matches the mix story only if “complex” is defined and the person over the ceiling goes somewhere that can hold them. The list does not say where. Points 4 and 5 price the job and then lock the price into pensions. They are a transfer. They are not a training plan, and training is the gap De Wilde states on VRT (“our security staff are not trained to work with these profiles”). The package never funds the skill it says is missing.

**mechanism_fit (0–10):** **5**

## 3. Options considered

| ID | Option | One-line effect |
|----|--------|-----------------|
| A | Status quo | Keep today’s DVZ centres, the minister’s claimed staffing push and the buildings already in the pipeline, including the 50-place Steenokkerzeel short-stay centre aimed at end-2026, with no new short-term placement rule. |
| B | Abolish / deregulate | Stop administrative detention in closed centres and do returns only on a voluntary path, or in prison when a criminal sentence still applies. |
| C | Price / tax reform | Index a hardship allowance to recorded incidents at centre level, time-limited, and keep it out of the pension base so the price tracks the risk. |
| D | Conditional transfer | Pay a temporary premium only on wings or shifts above a published incident threshold, and only while a supervisor ratio is actually met. |
| E | Public provision / invest | Staff and train a small clinical-and-security unit, add the camera cover staff in Brugge asked for, and separate short stay from long stay, inside the federal centres. |
| F | Pilot | One centre runs a written complex-profile ceiling for six months together with a named alternative bed and a public assault and length-of-stay table. |
| G | **This proposal** | ACV’s five points as stated: staff guarantee, no extra residents without supervisors, an undefined cap on complex profiles, higher risk and clothing allowances, and those allowances kept in the pension calculation. |

Option B is the real abolish case. Closed centres are a coercive instrument. If the state cannot hold people safely, ending administrative detention removes this workplace and this harm. It also removes the federal tool for forced return. People with no right to stay and no prison sentence would then be in the community or in a voluntary programme. That is a coherent policy. It is a different policy from the one the unions asked for. They want the centres to keep working under a safer roster, not to close.

Option A is what the minister effectively offered on 13 October: resources already freed, infrastructure queued with minister Matz, no new short-term rule the unions would accept. The 13 November 2025 government note on the Steenokkerzeel short-stay centre (50 places, four new centres planned at Zandvliet, Jumet, Jabbeke and Steenokkerzeel) is the medium-term building path she can point at. It does not staff tomorrow’s wing in Brugge or Merksplas.

## 5. Distribution & transfer constraint

- Winners: DVZ centre staff if the floor is real and the allowances rise; unions that bargain those premiums; residents in a given centre if the mix becomes calmer and the roster holds.
- Losers: people labelled “complex” who lose a place and have no named alternative; the return caseload the minister ranks first, if the cap is used to refuse criminal and nuisance files; taxpayers, through a higher wage bill and a pension base that compounds; other federal staff if hardship pay in closed centres becomes the precedent for pensionizing every risk premium.
- Exit / entry risk for productive base: indirect. This is not a tax on firms. A centre that cannot remove people with no right to stay shifts the cost onto reception, local policing and the informal labour market. That channel is real and unquantified here.
- New permanent clientele? Yes if the allowances enter the pension calculation. The entitlement then survives the incident rate that justified it. A headcount guarantee with no ratio and no sunset is a second ratchet.

**capture_risk (0–10):** **6**

## 7. Belgian competence & implementation

- Competent level: federal. Closed centres are DVZ, under the minister for Asylum and Migration. Placement rules and the DVZ roster sit there.
- EU constraint: return and detention are bounded by the EU return and reception rules and by Belgian aliens law. A cap cannot be a quiet way to release people the law says may be held, and it cannot be a quiet way to hold them in worse conditions. This memo does not restate that case law.
- Admin capacity / dual-structure friction: infrastructure is the Buildings Agency under minister Vanessa Matz, which Van Bossuyt says she has already asked to prioritise. Pension treatment of public allowances is federal pension and public-service law, not a DVZ circular. A five-point list aimed only at the migration minister splits across three pens. The quota brake and a defined cap can be written as operating rules. The premium and the pension base cannot.
- The strike was reported suspended on 14 Oct after talks with DVZ management. That is implementation noise. It is not a text of what was agreed.

**competence_fit (0–10):** **7**

## 9. Recommendation

`amend`

Minimum fix before this package is worth supporting:

1. Write the quota brake as a number: occupied places cannot rise unless a published supervisor ratio is met. Put the minister’s 846/550 claim next to the establishment plan so the baseline is checkable.
2. Define “complex profile” with clinical or security criteria, and name the bed a person gets when a centre is at its ceiling (medical unit, prison if a sentence applies, or another staffed federal place). No ceiling without that door.
3. Keep any hardship premium temporary and outside the pension base until DVZ publishes its annual cost and an incident rate. Do not pensionize it in this round.
4. Fund the training the union says is missing. The five points never do.
5. Publish assaults, self-harm and length of stay per centre. The cap is otherwise unfalsifiable.

Adopt the quota brake on its own if the rest cannot be specified. Reject the cap-and-pension pair as a single undifferentiated demand.
