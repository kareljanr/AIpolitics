## Executive judgment (fair)

After Assad’s fall, **return became a real option** for a subset of Syrians; 2025 already showed **219** Syrian voluntary returns vs **6** in 2024 (Fedasil). The 2026 reform does three sensible things at once:

1. **Time-price the exit** (degressive top-up: earlier voluntary return during first procedure → higher support).  
2. **Stop paying** reintegration to high-volume **visa-free / low-protection** destinations (Brazil, Moldova, Balkans, Georgia) except vulnerability cases — cutting an obvious misuse channel (those countries topped 2025 return lists).  
3. Deliver support largely as **reintegration plans in origin** (PRP in-kind + limited PAP cash), with **one-shot + entry-ban coupling** where possible to blunt revolving-door abuse.

This is not genius open-borders abolition nor pure cruelty theatre. It is a **mechanism-aware administrative reform** in a domain where Belgium’s costs are dominated by **reception duration**, not by the reintegration cheque itself. Residual risks: deadweight (pay people who would leave anyway), limited early take-up, and political “premie” framing that oversells cash.

**Support**, with hard KPI honesty.

## Steelman (extensive, good faith)

### 1. Reception is the expensive margin

- Housing an asylum seeker for months/years dwarfs a one-time ~€3–5k reintegration package.  
- Van Bossuyt (VRT): early rebuild in origin is “serieuze besparing” vs centre places — steelman holds **if** the marginal person exits earlier **because** of the incentive.

### 2. Degressive design is better than a flat “goodbye bonus”

- Coalition agreement foresaw degressive model; Fedasil implements as **top-up only** on base package (base not cut for late leavers in the same way).  
- Highest top-up while claim is live at CGVS/DVZ (**€2,000** in PDF schematic); drops at appeal (**€500**); zero after 60 days post-final refuse.  
- Aligns private timing with public cost: delay burns public euros.

### 3. Syria priority matches a regime shock, not ethnic favouritism as policy theory

- Priority country = **extra support for a designated origin** after a major political change that reopens return options.  
- Currently **only Syria** (Fedasil). Can be retargeted if conditions change — steelman: flexible country list beats permanent nationality entitlements.

### 4. Cutting Brazil/Moldova-class support attacks adverse selection

- 2025 top destinations included Brazil and Moldova; minister ended reintegration support there (except vulnerability).  
- Steelman: stop subsidising circular/economic mobility dressed as “return support.”

### 5. In-kind + partners + EU co-finance reduce pure cash leakage

- PRP via local partners (housing, micro-business, medical, job help); PAP cash limited window post-arrival.  
- Programme **EU co-financed** (Fedasil).  
- Entry ban “waar mogelijk” reduces repeat asylum shopping.

### 6. Complements forced return capacity

- Minister still pushes escorts, closed centres, readmission — voluntary track is **not** the sole tool. Steelman: dual track is standard European practice.

## 2. Mechanism

Named mechanisms:

1. **Price/timing incentive** (degressive top-up).  
2. **Eligibility pruning** (no PRP/PAP for selected countries).  
3. **In-kind reintegration** reducing failure-to-reintegrate → re-migration.  
4. **Entry ban** reducing moral hazard of paid exit then return.

**mechanism_fit: 7.0** — matches stated goals better than flat cash or pure rhetoric. Weakness: elasticity of Syrian (and other) early exit to €2k–5k is **empirical unknown**.

## 9. Recommendation

**`support`**

Minimum monitoring amendments (do not block):

1. Quarterly public: voluntary returns by nationality × procedure stage × days-to-exit.  
2. Publish **implied reception-day savings** methodology (even a range).  
3. Track **re-entry / new claims** after paid exit (entry-ban effectiveness).  
4. Keep country priority list **reviewable** with criteria, not permanent favourites.  
5. Independent 18-month evaluation vs 2024–25 baseline (Assad confound explicit).

Abolish option remains valid if take-up is pure deadweight — falsifier covers that.

## 10. Falsifier

> We reverse toward **reject/amend-down** if by end-2027 Fedasil/CGVS data show **no** reduction in median reception duration for eligible Syrian (and other degressive) cohorts vs matched pre-reform controls, **or** re-entry/new-claim rates after support exceed a published threshold proving revolving-door failure, **or** unit cost of support + admin exceeds estimated reception save.
