## Executive judgment (fair)

A real shock (energy prices after the Iran-war flare-up) met a **classic Belgian instrument failure**: voluntary employer-mediated support, complex temporary rules, **pre-finance then reclaim next tax year**, three-month window. Coalition nearly broke in April over an €80m package that, by late July, almost no firm uses.

This is **not** Smaakhaven-class prestige. The problem diagnosis has substance. The design is the clown. Temporary + capped + voluntary were the only redeeming design choices — and those same features, combined with admin lag, killed take-up. Score: **reject this instrument pattern**; do not recycle “tax credit next year for temporary cash pain.”

## Steelman (extensive, good faith)

### 1. Energy prices hurt workers who must drive

- After the 2026 Middle East escalation, fuel and energy costs spiked; car-dependent employees face a real cash-flow hit on commute.  
- Targeting **people who work** (not only transfers) matches Arizona’s work-oriented brand and avoids pure universal energy cheques.  
- Cap of **+10 ct/km** and **+20%** existing allowance keeps the subsidy from becoming an open-ended mobility TE.

### 2. Temporary + ceiling is more honest than open-ended crisis law

- Window **May–July 2026** only; Van Peteghem: re-evaluate after 3 months.  
- Total package **€80m** — small vs federal budget; VBO liked the time limit and spend cap.  
- Voluntary for employers avoids a new mandate on already-squeezed firms (UNIZO’s red line).

### 3. Multi-channel package, not only cars

- **€15m** via OCMW energy/stookolie funds for vulnerable fossil-heated households (summer timing acknowledged as limiting heat need).  
- Accise increase on fossil heating **deferred to 1 Aug**.  
- Self-employed: H1 2026 SSC **deferral** without rights loss (cash-flow, not gift).  
- Campaigns: energy saving, telework, carpool, CREG comparator — demand-side, near-zero fiscal cost.

### 4. Tax-free top-up for workers is clean incidence

- Extra km money not taxed at employee level → more net help per euro of employer cost (when the employer actually participates).  
- Existing-scheme increases fully fiscally compensated (up to caps) → theory: employer indifferent, employee gains.

### 5. Political economy of a thin majority

- Package nearly caused a **regeringscrisis**; landing any finite package preserved coalition capacity for larger fiscal consolidation. Steelman: better a small, time-boxed deal than collapse or a permanent new entitlement.

## 1. Problem claim

- **Metric / baseline:** Spike in household and firm energy/fuel costs spring 2026 (Iran-related supply shock + BE energy market structure). Commute cost for car-dependent employees; energy poverty for fossil-heated homes.  
- **Who is hurt:** Employees driving to work; SMEs with thin margins; vulnerable households on oil/gas; self-employed unable to pass through costs.  
- **Type:** temporary external price shock + partial market/state failure (tax wedge + delayed indexation dynamics amplify second-round costs).  
- **Truth of problem diagnosis:** **6.5 / 10** — shock is real; framing “workers + vulnerables” is fair; overstating that a 3-month km top-up is the right lever is not.  
- **Confidence:** **Medium** (press + employer orgs; no single FPB shock table used here).  
- **Sources:** VRT package articles; UNIZO cost-stack argument.

## 2. Mechanism

Named mechanisms:

1. **Cash-flow relief** for commute fuel cost via higher tax-free km allowance.  
2. **Employer as delivery channel** + **delayed tax credit** as public finance instrument.  
3. **Targeted hardship fund** (OCMW) for heating poor.  
4. **Information / soft demand** (campaigns, CREG).

Does the instrument attack the mechanism?

| Channel | Fit |
|---------|-----|
| OCMW €15m | Partial — right target, high access friction (VVSG), small vs need |
| Accise freeze | Better price-side tool; temporary |
| SSC deferral | Cash-flow only; bill returns |
| **Km tax credit** | **Poor** — voluntary + complex + pre-finance + 3-month then reverse → rational non-participation |

**mechanism_fit: 3.0**

Ex-post (Jul 2026): Attentia ~**4%** of clients raised allowance (mostly large firms); SD Worx ~**4%** SMEs, **8–9%** firms >50 staff, **~1%** micro (<5). UNIZO: little employee demand; hate temporary promise then claw back. Design predicted failure; data confirmed it. **Medium** evidence (HR-provider samples, not census).

## 5. Distribution & transfer constraint

- **Winners (intended):** Car-commuting employees at participating (mostly large) firms; some OCMW clients; self-employed who defer SSC.  
- **Losers:** Taxpayers funding envelope; non-participating workers (most); micro-firms with zero HR capacity; self-employed drivers excluded from km scheme (UNIZO/NSZ).  
- **Exit / productive base:** Low direct exit risk at €80m; **indirect** harm is precedent of crisis-layer admin and political capital spent on dead letter.  
- **New permanent clientele?** Risk medium-low if truly temporary; UNIZO warns unions may keep the higher allowance demand after window — classic ratchet.  
- **Capture:** Large employers with payroll systems capture most of the usable subsidy; SMEs do not.

**capture_risk: 4.0** (temporary limits lock-in; uneven access is the real inequity).

## 7. Belgian competence & implementation

- **Competent level:** Federal personal/corporate tax and SSC — correct for tax credit and social-contribution deferral. OCMW delivery is local with federal money — dual friction known.

- **Admin capacity:** Payroll + tax-credit lag is exactly where Belgian crisis tools die. Three-month temporary rules maximise fixed cost of understanding relative to benefit.

## 9. Recommendation

**`reject`** this instrument pattern.

If amend were forced for a future shock:

1. Prefer **automatic** household tools (tariff / cheque / temporary accise) with published unit cost.  
2. If employer channel: **instant** payroll credit or monthly VAT-style refund — never “pay now, reclaim next year.”  
3. No three-month promise that must be clawed back in staff communication.  
4. Publish take-up KPIs within 60 days; auto-sunset if take-up < threshold.

Default abundance path: fix structural energy and labour-tax costs; do not layer temporary lasagne.
