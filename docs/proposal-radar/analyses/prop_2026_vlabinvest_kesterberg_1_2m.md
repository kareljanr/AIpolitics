## Steelman (proponent’s best case)

Halle-Vilvoorde has a real care backlog. The province’s own agency says Flemish Brabant, and the Flemish Rand in particular, starts from a historical shortfall in welfare and health supply. A 2026 dashboard built on the provincial welfare report and VAPH data puts the arrondissement well under the Flemish average for disability support: fewer users of directly accessible help, fewer multifunction-centre clients, fewer adults with a persoonsvolgend budget. Land and houses are expensive. A provider that has to buy a building here pays a Rand premium before it houses anyone.

Kesterberg is the kind of building that premium usually destroys. It is a former assisted-living complex on the Ninoofsesteenweg in Leerbeek (Pajottegem), two wings from 2009 and 2019, 79 assisted flats and a concierge dwelling. It closed at the start of 2026. Empty, it is a candidate for ordinary housing. Occupied by a disability provider, the care layout is already partly there.

Vzw Wonen en Werken voor Personen met Autisme, known as De Okkernoot, buys it with a Vlabinvest investment subsidy and refits it as 68 contemporary flats. Forty-four are additional residential places. Twenty-four replace outdated rooms for people the organisation already supports. The director’s account of the move, in the regional press, is that those 24 leave Vollezele, gain several times the room they have now, and free that site for short stays so families can be relieved for a while. Collective rooms and the outdoor space are for daytime activity and for a link with the neighbourhood. The bike library in the garage can stay. The recognisable exterior stays as far as possible. Door widths, thresholds and showers are brought into line with disability rules and, on the organisation’s telling, with VIPA norms.

The public cheque is small next to the project. Vlabinvest’s published rule is a few percent of the accepted investment, a bit more when the project adds supply or cannot get a VIPA subsidy, plus 15 percent of the land share in the Flemish Rand, against an appraisal. Flanders gives the agency €2.35 million a year for this whole provincial job. Coppens, as chair, puts this file at about €1.2 million and the organisation’s project, works included, at about €13 million. He also says they will look at a renovation subsidy only once the plans are final. That is leverage on a closed care site, not a new institution on a greenfield, and not the province pretending to be the VAPH.

The people who gain are specific. Someone in this region who needs a staffed home and does not have one. A household that needs a short break. An existing resident leaving a room that is too small. The alternative is to leave the building to the housing market and to keep spending the same annual envelope on smaller files that do not add 44 residential places in the arrondissement where the reach is lowest.

## 1. Problem claim

- **Metric / baseline:**
  - **Reach, not a vibe.** ELZ Halle-Vilvoorde dashboard (June 2026), disability block, data year 2023, source marked VAPH, Flemish average without Brussels. Arrondissement Halle-Vilvoorde versus that average: rechtstreeks toegankelijke hulp 22.5 users per 10,000 inhabitants versus 42.2 (−47 percent); multifunction centre 31.0 clients per 10,000 youth aged 0–21 versus 65.6 (−53 percent); persoonsvolgend budget 39.4 persons per 10,000 adults 21+ versus 57.1 (−31 percent); personal-assistance budget for youth 7.5 per 10,000 aged 0–21 versus 15.7 (−52 percent). The same page says recognised persons with a disability are 38 per 1,000 inhabitants in the arrondissement versus 62 per 1,000 in Flanders.
  - **The waiting-list trap.** The same table shows a PVB waiting list of 19.0 per 10,000 adults 21+ versus 33.4. The dashboard’s own note says a lower list is not a good result: reach is lower because supply and referral are thinner, and people who are never referred never appear on a list. Coppens’ phrase “historische zorgachterstand” matches that reading. It does not match a claim that a long formal queue has been counted in Leerbeek.
  - **This building.** VRT, 19 October 2026: Kesterberg closed at the start of 2026; 79 assisted flats and a concierge dwelling; Halle-Vilvoorde has a large shortage of places for people with a disability. Editiepajot: address Ninoofsesteenweg 127; wings of 2009 and 2019; after works, an expected 68 flats, of which 44 are additional residential places and 24 are better housing for existing clients. Ring TV’s indexed text (20 October 2026; direct fetch failed): the organisation supports about 400 people with autism or an intellectual disability, about 130 of them residential, and receives two to three residential applications a week; 24 residents would move from Vollezele; that site would be used for short stays; first residents at Kesterberg aimed at within a year and a half.
  - **What is not a baseline.** No sourced count of how many of those weekly applications have a VAPH budget, a priority group, or a Halle-Vilvoorde address. No occupancy date in a permit. The Flanders-wide PVB queue is a different instrument and is not used here as this file’s deficit.
- **Who is hurt:** Adults and youth in Halle-Vilvoorde who need residential disability support and do not get it, and households that carry that support unaided. Existing De Okkernoot residents in the rooms described as outdated. Taxpayers, for the subsidy. Other 2026 care-infrastructure applicants, if this one file crowds the annual envelope. Future residents, if the rooms are bought and the operating budget never arrives.
- **Type:** state failure (a long regional shortfall in disability reach, plus a land-price wedge in the Rand) mixed with a missing market response (a closed care building is worth more as housing than as a specialised home unless someone bridges the purchase). It is also a transfer: a public capital grant to one provider.
- **Truth of problem diagnosis (0–10):** **7.0**
- **Confidence:** **Strong** that Halle-Vilvoorde’s disability reach sits under the Flemish average on the 2023 rates above, and that Vlabinvest’s mandate is to spend a Flemish infrastructure envelope against that backlog. **Medium** that Kesterberg closed at the start of 2026 and that the plan is 44 additional residential places plus 24 relocations. **Weak** on “two to three applications a week,” on the 400 / 130 headcount, and on any claim that those applications are the same thing as a funded waiting list.
- **Sources:** VRT NWS 19 Oct 2026; Editiepajot (screenshot embedded on the page is dated 20 Oct 2026); Ring TV indexed text 19–20 Oct 2026; ELZ Halle-Vilvoorde dashboard; Vlabinvest subsidy page (indexed).

The backlog is real. The sentence “therefore this building, these 44 places, this cheque” is a proposal, not a measurement. The lower formal waiting list is the fact a honest diagnosis has to keep in the same paragraph as the shortfall.

## 2. Mechanism

Named mechanism(s):

1. **Purchase subsidy.** Vlabinvest grants an investment subsidy so the vzw can buy the site. Editiepajot states €1,232,536.76, granted in June 2026. VRT quotes Coppens: “We investeren via Vlabinvest zo’n 1,2 miljoen euro in de aankoop van de Kesterberg.” The legal form in the local write-up and on Vlabinvest’s own LinkedIn excerpt is an investeringssubsidie, not a provincial purchase. The vzw becomes the owner.
2. **Published percentage, not a negotiated lump.** Official subsidy page and the 2026 basiswerkingsregels (indexed): 5 percent of the accepted investment, plus 3 percent if the project cannot claim VIPA, plus 2 percent on the part that creates additional supply, plus a 0.5 percent general-cost slice (5 percent of a 10 percent general-cost base). In the Flemish Rand, an extra 15 percent of the accepted amount of the bare land or the land share. A building purchase needs a split appraisal by a recognised valuer. A land buy that is out of proportion to the investment plan is subsidised pro rata. Eligible sector list includes persons with a disability. Halle-Vilvoorde is inside the Vlaamse Rand area. Award rounds are June (files in by the end of April) and December (files in by the end of October). A €500,000 annual reserve is for small files (under 15 disability places). This file, at 44 additional places, is not that reserve.
3. **Reuse plus a quality move.** Forty-four net new residential places. Twenty-four existing clients moved into better rooms. On the Ring account, Vollezele then becomes short stay. VRT’s quote from Coppens is looser: the 24 renewed places “create extra capacity and improve the operation.” That can mean the short-stay reuse. It can also be heard as 24 more beds. The stricter text is Editiepajot: the 24 are existing clients.
4. **A second cheque, not yet granted.** Editiepajot: total project cost, works included, estimated at about €13 million. Once plans are final, De Okkernoot wants to ask Vlabinvest for further support for the renovation. That amount is not decided. It is not in the fiscal columns.
5. **What the subsidy does not buy.** A persoonsvolgend budget, a recognition of 44 extra VAPH places, or the wages of the staff. The 2026 checklist (indexed) asks the applicant to declare that a purchased building can be in operation within five years. That is a clock. It is not an operating budget.

Does the instrument actually attack that mechanism?

The infrastructure mechanism, yes. In a Rand market, a closed care building is bid away unless someone cheapens the purchase for a care buyer. A small percentage plus a land-share top-up, tied to additional supply and to an appraisal, is aimed at that wedge. Reuse of a 2009/2019 care shell is a better attack than a new campus.

The care mechanism, only halfway. A place is a room plus a funded support trajectory. This euro pays toward the room. Flanders pays the trajectory, through the VAPH, under a different budget and a different queue. The announcement, especially the VRT lead (“the province invests €1.2 million… 44 extra places”), speaks as if the provincial cheque is what opens the places. The fuller local text is more careful and still does not name the operating source.

The incentive inside the percentage is mostly the right way up: more subsidy for additional supply and for Rand land, less double-dipping with VIPA, a valuation, a pro-rata brake on a land-heavy buy. The remaining bend is ordinary cost-plus. A dearer accepted investment draws a larger cheque. Nothing retrieved in the decision shows a clawback if the 44 places are never funded.

**mechanism_fit (0–10):** **6.0**

## 3. Options considered

| ID | Option | One-line effect |
|----|--------|-----------------|
| A | Status quo | No purchase subsidy. Kesterberg stays on the market. De Okkernoot keeps its current rooms. The €1.23 million stays in the 2026 care-infrastructure round for other files. The reach gap stays. |
| B | Abolish / deregulate | Stop Vlabinvest’s care-infrastructure subsidies, or refuse this file, and leave the purchase to the vzw, to VIPA, and to its own lenders. The Rand land top-up disappears. If the deal needs this €1.23 million to close, the 44 places do not appear. If the vzw can close anyway, the public euro was not the marginal euro. |
| C | Price / tax reform | Do not cheapen the building. Change the VAPH allocation so a persoonsvolgend budget is easier to obtain and to spend in Halle-Vilvoorde, including on rent. That attacks the operating queue and the “money follows historical use” loop. It does not, by itself, keep this building in care use. |
| D | Conditional transfer | Keep a capital grant, but pay or claw back against occupied, VAPH-funded places, and publish the appraisal split before the second cheque. The buyer still gets help with the Rand price. The province stops counting unfunded rooms as places. |
| E | Public provision / invest | Vlabinvest or another public landlord buys Kesterberg and leases it to a licensed provider. The public balance sheet holds the asset. The operating-budget problem is unchanged, and the province becomes a care landlord. |
| F | Pilot | Subsidise a first wing only (the 24 relocations, or a defined slice of the 44) and release the rest when a written operating path exists. Slower. Smaller stranded-asset risk. |
| G | **This proposal** | June 2026 investment subsidy of €1,232,536.76 toward the purchase. Plan: 44 additional residential places, 24 better rooms for current clients, works estimated at about €13 million, renovation subsidy to be requested later. |

A is honest about the envelope: this file is large inside a €2.35 million year. B tests whether the subsidy is marginal or decorative. C is the better tool for the queue and the wrong tool for “this building closes next month.” D is the amend. E concentrates ownership without solving wages. F is the cautious version of G. G is what was granted.

## 5. Distribution & transfer constraint

- Winners: De Okkernoot, as buyer, receives a capital grant on the order of half of one annual provincial care-infrastructure envelope (€1,232,536.76 / €2,350,000 ≈ 52 percent). Future residents if the 44 places open with a real support budget. The 24 people whose rooms improve. Households if Vollezele actually becomes short stay. The seller of Kesterberg, who finds a buyer for a closed scheme. The neighbourhood, if a dead complex is lived in and the bike library stays.
- Losers: Other eligible care projects in the same June round, to the extent the envelope is binding. Taxpayers, for a one-off €1.23 million. A later tenant of ordinary housing on that site, if the alternative was homes. No wage effect is claimed or scored.
- Exit / entry risk for the productive base: Negligible. This does not tax work, and it does not close a market. It shifts one building from assisted living for older people (already closed) to disability support. The productive-base risk is opportunity cost inside a small envelope, not an exit of firms.
- New permanent clientele? The subsidy is one-off, but the relationship is not. The same provider is invited by its own announcement to come back for the renovation. A residential service, once open, will also need yearly VAPH money. That operating clientele is Flemish, not created by this decision, and it is the point of the places. The capture that belongs to this instrument is the capital relationship: one vzw, one site, a chair who fronts the press moment, and an open second ask.

**capture_risk (0–10):** **5.0**

Published rates, an appraisal rule, a small-file reserve, and a scoring logic that prefers extra supply, backlog sectors and the Rand are real brakes. They are not, on the record retrieved here, shown to have been applied in public to this file. Half a year’s pot to one buyer, with a renovation ask already previewed, is why capture is not a 2.

## 7. Belgian competence & implementation

- Competent level: Infrastructure subsidies for care buildings in Flemish Brabant are Vlabinvest’s job, under a Flemish assignment of €2.35 million a year. Persons with a disability are an eligible sector. Leerbeek (Pajottegem) is in the Vlaamse Rand area, so the land-share rule can apply. The VAPH, not the province, recognises and funds the support. The municipality is not the payer.
- EU constraint: No notification, no state-aid file, and no VIPA decision was retrieved. A small social-infrastructure grant inside a standing envelope is not, on its face, an EU quota fight. This memo does not invent a state-aid clearance or a breach.
- Admin capacity / dual-structure friction: The building was assisted living. Disability rules differ. VRT’s director says more level thresholds, accessible showers and wider doors are still required. Editiepajot says the rooms already meet disability rules and that the heavy work is in the collective spaces, and that the result should meet VIPA norms. Both can be true. The +3 percent “no VIPA” add-on means the file must also say whether VIPA is in or out. That choice was not published in the articles. The five-year operation declaration and the 20-year real-right test are rules of the scheme, not facts checked against this deed. Coppens’ “within a year and a half” (via Ring) is faster than the five-year outer clock and is still only a press intention.

**competence_fit (0–10):** **8.0**

The body that wrote the cheque is the body Flanders set up to write this kind of cheque. The overreach is rhetorical: a provincial capital grant described as if it were the creation of staffed places.

## 9. Recommendation

`amend`

Keep the June 2026 purchase subsidy. It is the right kind of tool for a closed care building in the Rand. The minimum fix, before any renovation euro:

1. Publish the decision: accepted investment base, rate stack, land-share versus building, appraisal, and whether the +3 percent “no VIPA” rate was used.
2. State the operating path for the 44 places (recognition and budget, or an explicit statement that the rooms wait on a VAPH trajectory). Do not pay a renovation subsidy on rooms that have no such path.
3. Count three numbers separately in the next press moment: 44 net new residential places, 24 relocations, and any short-stay places at Vollezele. Do not add them into one “extra places” figure.

Abolish (option B) would be the right call only if the vzw could buy the building without this euro, or if the June round had a higher-scoring file that this grant displaced. Neither fact is on the record. Do nothing (option A) saves the €1.23 million and leaves the building’s care use to chance.
