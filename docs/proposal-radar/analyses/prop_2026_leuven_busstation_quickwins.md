## Steelman (proponent’s best case)

The bus station on Leuven’s Martelarenplein is the place where a train passenger becomes a bus passenger, and it is failing at that handoff. VRT calls it the busiest bus station in Flanders and puts the volume at 45,000 travellers a day. De Lijn’s head of doorstroming, Dries Wathion, says the same thing in kind: it is the busiest station in Flanders, the number of travellers is still rising, and a driver there has to watch pedestrians, cyclists, other traffic and crossing buses at once.

The harm is not theoretical. In February 2024 a 27-year-old student, named by Leuven Actueel as Richa Mishra, was killed at the station. ROBtv reported on 4 September 2025 that the court gave the driver a suspended sentence of three months and found that she did not have priority on the coloured suggestion strip. In August 2026 a 75-year-old man was caught under a bus on that same green strip and badly hurt in both legs. Local press links further serious crashes at the station after the 2024 death. A driver told VRT in the summer of 2026 that practice at the strip is “give and take” and “survival of the fittest.” Another described hurried people coming out of the railway tunnel and stepping onto the bus lane after about three metres, exactly where the bus is turning.

The city’s own earlier answer helped create the misunderstanding. The green strips, with continuous white lines and arrows, were painted in 2024 after the student’s death. Green reads as “you may walk.” A suggestion strip is not a zebra. Pedestrians still had to yield to De Lijn buses turning in. ROBtv’s street conversations in September 2025, which are a canvas and not a poll, found traveller after traveller who thought the green surface gave them priority. Schepen Dirk Vansina (CD&V) now says that out loud: the green gave people the feeling of a safe walking zone, while the buses have priority. The blue paint, the removal of the continuous white lines, and the new danger pictograms are meant to say one sentence: you may step here, and you do not have priority.

The rest of the package attacks the geometry that paint cannot fix. Early November, guiding rails at the station tunnel and the Diestsevest are supposed to send people to a designated walk and crossing instead of letting them spill out anywhere between the platforms and the square. Temporary fences toward one crossing were already reported on the Diestsevest side of the tunnel from the Monday before 20 October. Tactile tiles are planned for visually impaired travellers. The narrow footpath along the wall at platform 1 is closed, so people can only alight at the front of that stop. Part of the glass balustrade comes down, the info column is gone, and the bin moves, so people who have just got off have somewhere to stand that is not the bus lane.

The route change is the piece that matches what drivers have been saying. Buses that arrive via the Tiensevest, the Maria-Theresiastraat and the Bondgenotenlaan, and that used to terminate on platform 1, go on to platform 15 by the Dirk Bouts building. They no longer take the sharp right turn onto platform 1, which Vansina calls the place where the buses stand most sideways and the drivers see pedestrians worst. After the rails are in, De Lijn trials a loop around Plein van de Vriendschap so buses enter the odd platforms 3 to 9 more straight on, see crossing people earlier, and make fewer dangerous right turns. A temporary stop sign and stop line for traffic coming from the Diestsevest give those buses priority during the trial. City and De Lijn then evaluate whether the circulation stays and whether more markings are needed. Police supervision was announced with the 26 October start.

None of this is sold as the final station. Vansina and De Lijn say the autumn works sit beside a study, already started, on how to organise the bus infrastructure and the flows, including spreading stops toward the Acerta building or the former depot. The Martelarenplein rebuild, which the press dates from 2027 and in one VRT line from mid-2027, is supposed to put pedestrian guidance at the centre. The best case for this package is modest and sequential: stop teaching the wrong priority with a green carpet, get bodies out of the worst blind turn before the square is rebuilt, and use the trial as evidence for that rebuild. A station this busy cannot wait for the study before it moves the sharp corner.

## 1. Problem claim

- **Metric / baseline:** No crash table, exposure rate or near-miss series was retrieved. The public record is a set of events and descriptions. (1) Fatal crash, February 2024, 27-year-old student Richa Mishra (VRT report dated 19 Feb 2024; named by Leuven Actueel). (2) ROBtv, 4 Sep 2025: the court found she had no priority on the coloured suggestion strip; the driver received three months suspended. Judgment text not retrieved. (3) August 2026: a 75-year-old man seriously hurt in both legs on the green strip (VRT, 13 Oct 2026; the 14 Oct piece says a man in his seventies). (4) Leuven Actueel links a later serious crash in which an 80-year-old was hit by a bus; that article was not read in full. (5) VRT’s volume line is 45,000 travellers a day and “busiest in Flanders.” Wathion confirms “busiest” and rising numbers, and does not state 45,000 in the quote used here. (6) N-VA’s label “most dangerous bus station in the country” is an opposition phrase after the August crash, not a national ranking. (7) ROBtv, 4 Sep 2025, street canvas: people on the green strip believed they had priority. Headline “bijna niemand” is the outlet’s gloss on who they spoke to. It is not a poll percentage and is not used as one.
- **Who is hurt:** Pedestrians hit or nearly hit, above all at platform 1 beside the railway-tunnel mouth. Drivers who carry a death or a serious injury. Travellers whose terminus moves to platform 15. Visually impaired passengers, whom the tactile tiles are meant to serve. Other traffic held at the temporary stop line during the route trial.
- **Type:** state failure in the design and signing of a public interchange (a non-crossing painted to look like a safe walk, plus a blind turn at a tunnel mouth). Secondary preference fight over who yields, pedestrian or bus.
- **Truth of problem diagnosis (0–10):** **7**
- **Confidence:** **Medium** that serious bus-pedestrian crashes have recurred at this station and that the green strip taught a priority the law did not give. The events are reported by VRT, Leuven Actueel and ROBtv, and the schepen accepts the false-impression point. **Weak** on how large a share of the crashes is colour confusion rather than sightline, crowding and distraction. Dirk Lauwers (UGent) and Vlaams Belang fractieleider Britt Huybrechts both say the crashes were not only people who misread the rule. **Weak** on the 45,000 figure and on any ranking of this station against others. No police or De Lijn statistical release was retrieved.
- **Sources:** VRT NWS 13 Oct 2026; VRT NWS 14 Oct 2026; Leuven Actueel 13 Oct 2026; ROBtv 4 Sep 2025; ROBtv 14 Oct 2026; Leuven Actueel 14 Oct 2026 (N-VA and Vlaams Belang); Leuven Actueel 20 Oct 2026 (temporary fences); Leuven Actueel 21 Oct 2026 (26 Oct route start); Leuven Actueel 7 Oct 2026 (minister De Ridder on the study and measures already taken).

## 2. Mechanism

Named mechanism(s):

1. **False priority cue.** A filled coloured strip with arrows is read as a walking zone or a zebra. The legal pedestrian crossing in the Code van de openbare weg is a row of white rectangles. Permanent markings in the teaching summaries of that code are white; yellow or orange is for works. A green or blue carpet is not that sign. ROBtv’s report of the 2025 verdict matches the city’s later admission: people thought they had priority, and the court said the victim did not.
2. **Blind conflict at the tunnel mouth.** People exit the NMBS tunnel and meet a bus that is turning sharply onto platform 1, often sideways to the pedestrian. Drivers say they cannot see them in time. This is a sightline and path-crossing problem.
3. **Scattered desire lines.** Pedestrians move at many points between the platforms and the Martelarenplein, crossing buses, cyclists and motor traffic. Rails and a single crossing try to gather those lines where both sides can see.
4. **Crowding into the lane.** A narrow footpath and a glass balustrade leave too little standing room, so people step into the bus path. Removing part of the balustrade and closing the narrow path are capacity moves at the stop.
5. **Attention.** Vansina’s sentence for the blue paint is that it should “aansporen tot meer oplettendheid.” Lauwers’s reply is that blue is also a strong colour that suggests priority, and that research showing a real difference between green and blue is scarce. Smartphone distraction is his general claim. It is not a coded cause of these Leuven crashes in the sources used here.

Does the instrument actually attack that mechanism?

The pictograms and the removal of the continuous white lines attack mechanism 1 better than a change of hue does. They try to state the rule the green carpet hid. The hue itself is a weak instrument: it keeps a filled, vivid strip in the bus path, which is the feature people already over-read. Lauwers’s doubt on that point is the right doubt. Whether the new marking has a basis in the road code is his claim (“die wettelijke basis ontbreekt”); this pass did not find a Leuven sign plan or police regulation that gives the blue strip a defined meaning, and it did not find the blue strip in the marking catalogue checked on wegcode.be. His aside on US practice (bright colours forbidden on crosswalks; softer tints recommended) was not checked against the Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices.

The route shift attacks mechanism 2 directly for the buses that leave platform 1. A bus that no longer makes that turn cannot hit someone in that turn. The Plein van de Vriendschap loop is aimed at the same mechanism for platforms 3, 5, 7 and 9: a straighter entry and a better view. It is a trial, with an evaluation promised and no metric published in advance.

The rails attack mechanism 3 if they gather people at one visible crossing. VRT’s 13 Oct line says the rails steer travellers toward the pedestrian strip. Vansina on ROBtv says they lead people to the Martelarenplein, “where one can cross,” at the narrowest and therefore safest point. Those are different funnels. Guiding people onto the shared strip reproduces the conflict. Guiding them to one crossing where they can see the bus reduces it. The 20 Oct fences are described as leading to one fixed crossing. Which geometry is on the ground after the metal rails replace the fences is not in a plan retrieved here.

Mechanism 4 is a real, small fit: more standing room and a closed trap-path. Mechanism 5 is the part the package does not earn. Repainting does not change the payoff for a person who has thirty seconds to catch a train, and it does not separate a body from a bus. Lauwers’s rule, “scheiden, scheiden, scheiden,” and N-VA’s side-platform idea are the instruments that match a pedestrian-bus conflict of this size. The autumn package leaves the shared surface in place and moves some of the traffic off it.

**mechanism_fit (0–10):** **5.5**

## 3. Options considered

| ID | Option | One-line effect |
|----|--------|-----------------|
| A | Status quo | Leave the 2024 green strip, the sharp turn onto platform 1, and the open tunnel mouth. Walking-pace rule, campaigns and the glass screen behind the platform-1 pole, already in place before this package, stay as they are. |
| B | Abolish / deregulate | Remove the coloured suggestion strip so the surface no longer reads as a walking zone, and leave priority to the ordinary road code: no invented carpet, no new rails, buses yield only at a real zebra or signal. |
| C | Price / tax reform | Give the pedestrian a legal zebra or a signal at the one crossing the rails create, so the delay sits on the bus, which can injure, and the rule on the ground matches the rule people already believe. No new tax is proposed or scored. |
| D | Conditional transfer | Peak-hour guides at the tunnel mouth, kept only while platform-1 injury crashes stay above a published line and removed when they fall. Hours, not a new subsidy scheme. |
| E | Public provision / invest | Build the separation the study is supposed to test: central islands or side platforms, or spread termini to the Acerta side and the former depot, and lock that geometry into the Martelarenplein works. |
| F | Pilot | Run the platform-15 shift and the straighter approach for a pre-registered window, count injury crashes and conflicts before touching the colour, and publish the evaluation the city already promises. |
| G | **This proposal** | Autumn 2026 package as announced: green strip painted blue with danger pictograms and white lines removed; guiding rails and tactile tiles; narrow platform-1 footpath closed; part of the glass balustrade, the info column and the bin moved; terminating buses from Tiensevest, Maria-Theresiastraat and Bondgenotenlaan to platform 15; trial loop via Plein van de Vriendschap for platforms 3–9; temporary stop control from the Diestsevest; structural study and 2027 square works left as a separate track. |

## 5. Distribution & transfer constraint

- Winners: Pedestrians and drivers at platform 1, if the sharp turn actually disappears and people stop stepping out of the tunnel at random. Visually impaired travellers, if the tactile tiles are placed on the path they use. The college and De Lijn, who can show a visible response before the 2027 works.
- Losers: Passengers whose bus now ends at platform 15 and who walk further with luggage. People who used the narrow platform-1 footpath. Drivers held at the temporary stop line on the Diestsevest side. Anyone who trusted the green carpet and now has to relearn a strip that is still not a zebra.
- Exit / entry risk for productive base: Small. A dangerous interchange nudges some travellers off the bus; this package is too small to move firms or residents. The risk that matters is sticking a shared strip into the 2027 square so the conflict becomes the new permanent layout.
- New permanent clientele? No subsidy, no new agency, no ongoing grant. Paint and rails are one-off works. The route pattern can become the timetable by default, which is an operational habit, not a captured rent.

**capture_risk (0–10):** **3**

The capture that exists is political substitution: a photogenic repaint can stand in for separation when the square is designed. It does not create a clientele.

## 7. Belgian competence & implementation

- Competent level: The square, the footpaths and the markings sit with the city. Where the buses stop, and which line uses which platform, sits with De Lijn, a Flemish operator. The schepen and De Lijn’s head of doorstroming announcing one package is the right pair. Minister De Ridder’s answer, as reported on 7 Oct 2026, places the structural study with the city and De Lijn and records short-term talks with the city and the police. That is the Flemish minister’s lane, used as oversight of the operator, not as a substitute for the local works.
- EU constraint: None that binds this package. Vehicle and road-safety rules sit in the background. They do not decide the colour of a Leuven strip.
- Admin capacity / dual-structure friction: City, De Lijn, NMBS tunnel, police and, from 2027, the square’s designers all touch the same few metres. The autumn package can be done by the first three. The failure mode is a route trial that expires while the square works lock in the old shared strip. Lauwers’s legality point, if a later sign-plan review supports it, is a federal road-code constraint on the city: a marking that is not in the code does not become a rule because it is blue.

**competence_fit (0–10):** **7.5**

## 9. Recommendation

`amend`

Keep the part that matches the harm. The buses that took the sharp right turn onto platform 1 should stay off that turn, and the straighter approach to platforms 3–9 should run as a real trial. Publish, before anyone calls it a success, the injury crashes and the conflicts at platform 1 for a comparable period before and after 26 October 2026, against the rest of this station and against other large De Lijn stations.

Stop using the colour as the safety claim. Either remove the filled strip (option B) or install a marking the road code already defines and then follow that rule (option C, a zebra or a signal where the bus must yield). A blue carpet with a pictogram that says “you have no priority” still invites people onto a surface the bus is allowed to drive through.

Do not let this package become the design brief for the Martelarenplein. The study’s decision, before works fix the geometry, should be separation: islands, side platforms, or termini that are spread so pedestrians and buses do not share the same strip. The autumn trial is evidence for that choice.

## 10. Falsifier

> We reverse this assessment if, within 12 months after 26 October 2026, injury crashes between buses and pedestrians at the Leuven bus station fall sharply versus the previous 12 months and versus other large De Lijn stations, and a published sightline check shows the new approach gives drivers an earlier view of a crossing pedestrian than the old platform-1 turn.
