## Executive judgment

Company cars are among Belgium’s largest, most regressive **tax expenditures**. Ending deductibility for new ICE (and tightening hybrids) is rare genuine structural reform. Clown low; genius high but not 9 because EV path still subsidises high earners’ mobility and modal shift to transit is incomplete.

---

## Steelman

1. **TE was huge and unfair.** Benefit concentrated on employees who get cars; paid by general taxation including non-car workers.  
2. **Externality.** Combustion fleets → local pollution + CO₂; price signal via corporate deductibility was inverted.  
3. **FPB-type evidence.** Ex-ante work finds large electrification acceleration and material net revenue improvement into the late 2020s.  
4. **Second-hand EV channel.** Corporate fleet turnover can flood used EV market (abundance for lower incomes) — contingent on volumes.

---

## Critique

### Residual problems

| Issue | Severity |
|-------|----------|
| EV still deductible (declining path) | Soft TE remains for high earners |
| Wage packaging: cash vs car | Firms may shift to other fringes |
| Charging / grid / company parking | Implementation friction |
| Border / lease timing games | Transitional avoidance |

### Options

A keep ICE TE · B **this greening path** · C kill all company-car TE including EV, raise net wages · D congestion pricing instead · E ban ICE sales only.

**C** is purer abundance/fiscal; **B** is achievable second-best already law.

### Distribution

Losers: employees who valued cheap ICE company cars; some lease sector transitional profits.  
Winners: taxpayers; urban air quality; eventually used-EV buyers.

---

## Recommendation

**`support`.** Prefer eventual cash-out of remaining EV TE into lower labour taxes. Do not reopen hybrid hole (see sister proposal).

**Falsifier:** by 2029, net fiscal + second-hand access + externality package worse than counterfactual in FPB-style evaluation.
