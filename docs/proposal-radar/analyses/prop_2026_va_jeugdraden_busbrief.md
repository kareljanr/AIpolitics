## Steelman (proponent’s best case)

A study choice should not depend on whether the bus still reaches the campus. In vervoerregio Vlaamse Ardennen, De Lijn changed 23 of 46 lines on 1 July 2026, and the operator’s own release says six lines were scrapped. The youth councils of the region, with the Flemish Youth Council, wrote to the minister who holds the mobility budget and asked her to look at that package again.

The people who feel it are not the ones who can choose a car. Many secondary and higher-education students in these municipalities go to Gent. A missing trip means a longer journey, a more expensive alternative, or a room in the city. A kot is a real cost, and it is not available to every family. The same bus is how a young person gets to a student job, a sports club, youth work, a shop, a doctor, or a friend. Young people with a disability have fewer substitutes. Parents who can drive are an advantage, not a public network.

The councils are not asking for a tram in every village. They ask for three things: review the cancellations and the limits in this region; invest again in rural access so a young person here has a comparable chance to one in the city; and put the daily users, including youth and local organisations, in the room when the network is cut. They offer a concrete meeting: ride the bus from Zwalm to Gent with them and talk on the way.

De Lijn says the cuts hit the least-occupied trips and that some alternatives remain. The councils’ point is that an average load can look “too empty” in a spread-out region and still be the only way a student leaves the village on a school morning. A method that sorts on average maximum occupancy, then lands hardest on the rural region, will keep producing that result. Reviewing the package, and buying back the trips that actually connect people to school and campus, is how you stop a saving on paper from becoming a smaller set of possible lives. The invitation to take the bus is a way to make the minister see the trip, not a substitute for the review.

## 1. Problem claim

- **Metric / baseline:**
  - **What changed.** De Lijn, 23 April 2026: from 1 July 2026, 23 of 46 lines in vervoerregio Vlaamse Ardennen are adjusted (50 percent). Six lines are scrapped. Other changes are a shorter route, fewer trips, no service on every day, or school-hour timing. The letter uses the same 23-of-46 figure and calls the package cancellations and limits. The Flemish Parliament question text for 12 November 2026 repeats: since 1 July 2026, 23 of 46 lines adjusted, of which 6 fully scrapped. The committee answer was not retrieved.
  - **What “scrapped” names.** The release’s lead says six lines. The body explicitly withdraws lijn 2 (Ronse), lijn 44 (Meilegem–Zottegem), lijn 408 (Gent Zuid–Munkzwalm–Brakel), lijn 427 (Oudenaarde–Zwijnaarde–Gent), and the Nachtraaf flex service in Oudenaarde. This memo does not invent a sixth name. Several other lines lose trips, Sunday service, market extras, or holiday service. A few changes add service: lijn 1 is extended through Stookt and gains Sunday; lijn 48 (Gent region) is extended to Zottegem at an hourly frequency as the substitute for lijn 44.
  - **Named substitutes, not a blank map.** Lijn 2 → lijn 20. Lijn 44 → extended lijn 48. Sunday on lijnen 40, 45 and 58 → De Lijn Flex. Several peak trips are retimed to school hours rather than removed. The letter does not discuss those substitutes.
  - **Why those lines.** De Lijn: H1 2025 data, average maximum occupancy first, then network structure and alternatives. Regions with more low-performing lines contribute more. VRT, 29 January 2026, quotes De Lijn (Demerling) on lines with average maximum occupancy under 8 passengers, and quotes the minister that the relevant figure is average maximum occupancy morning, midday and evening, sometimes three or four, not “eight boardings at every stop.” That threshold is press. It is not in the April release and not in the letter.
  - **Euro context, not this ask.** VRT, 13 February 2026, quotes vervoerregioraad chair David Vandekerkhove: Flanders-wide €35.5 million, and more than €3 million for the Flemish Ardennes; about €13 per inhabitant against about €5 on average, and €5 in Antwerp and €3 in Gent. VRT, 29 January 2026, says the region would carry 15 percent of the saving. The letter states no euro. Those figures are not the cost of this proposal.
  - **Harm claims without a count.** The letter says Gent is harder to reach, journeys get longer, alternatives cost more, a kot is not always possible, rural–urban inequality widens, isolation and mental-health risk rise, and the cuts push young people toward the car against Flemish climate goals. No student count, no journey-time table, no mode-shift figure.
- **Who is hurt:** Students and other young people in the signing municipalities who used a trip that was scrapped or thinned and who have no car, no licence, or no parent who can drive. The letter names young people with a disability as more dependent. Households that absorb the trip by driving, paying for a kot, or dropping a campus. Riders of the remaining full buses, if a reopen moves driver-hours back onto empty trips. Taxpayers, if “invest again” means the July kilometres return with no occupancy rule.
- **Type:** state failure (a public network sorted on thin occupancy, in a region the operator says had more such lines) mixed with a real access constraint for people who cannot drive. It is also a transfer fight over who keeps the rural kilometre.
- **Truth of problem diagnosis (0–10):** **6.0**
- **Confidence:** **Strong** that 23 of 46 lines changed on 1 July 2026 and that the letter asks for a review, new rural investment, and a say for youth. **Medium** that six lines were fully withdrawn and that Gent corridors 408 and 427 are among them. **Weak** on journey times, on how many students changed a study choice, on isolation and climate effects, and on the euro split (€3 million-plus versus 15 percent).
- **Sources:** Vlaamse Jeugdraad letter, 20 Oct 2026; VRT NWS, 19 Oct 2026; De Lijn release, 23 Apr 2026; VRT NWS, 29 Jan 2026 and 13 Feb 2026; Flemish Parliament committee agenda, 12 Nov 2026 (questions 434 and 468). Agenda text only.

The diagnosis that a rural student without a car lost options is credible, and De Lijn’s own list shows two Gent lines withdrawn. The diagnosis that 23 adjustments equal 23 lost futures, or that study choice now depends on the bus, is not shown. The letter treats adjustments, limits and cancellations as one injury.

## 2. Mechanism

Named mechanism(s):

1. **Reopen the July package.** “Herbekijk de afschaffingen en beperkingen” in the Flemish Ardennes. No list of trips, no occupancy floor, no date.
2. **Buy rural access back.** “Investeer opnieuw in de bereikbaarheid van landelijke regio’s,” with the standard that rural youth get the same chances as youth in cities. No euro, no kilometre, no definition of “the same.”
3. **A seat at the table.** Youth and local organisations join mobility decisions so the timetable matches daily use. The vervoerregioraad (the 15 local governments) already received the proposal, could offer alternatives, and saw some alternatives kept. De Lijn says that round ended with the measures discussed at the end of March, and that delay past 1 July would have required deeper cuts.
4. **A demonstrated trip.** The signatories invite the minister to ride from Zwalm to Gent with young people. That is a meeting format. Lijn 408, the Gent Zuid–Munkzwalm–Brakel line, is one of the withdrawn lines. This memo did not check the route planner for a remaining Zwalm–Gent path.

Does the instrument actually attack that mechanism?

The harm mechanism, where it is real, is a missing trip with no workable substitute for someone who cannot drive, especially toward school and Gent. A review attacks that only if it is bound to those trips. As written, “herbekijk” covers market extras, Sunday lines that De Lijn points to Flex, school-hour retiming, and route changes that sometimes add service. The vervoerregioraad already had the formal review. A second undifferentiated review does not add a rule.

“Investeer opnieuw” matches the access problem only if the euro buys the trips students use. An equal-chance standard against cities can also buy frequency a low-density region will not fill. De Lijn’s stated method was the opposite sort: move effort toward demand, and use flex where the fixed bus is thin. The letter does not say what happens to flex, to the under-8 test, or to the 2026 saving if the kilometres return.

The seat for youth is a process. It does not put a bus on the road. It can improve the next list. It can also become a local veto on any thin line. The bus ride is a way to show one corridor. It is not an operating plan.

**mechanism_fit (0–10):** **3.5**

## 3. Options considered

| ID | Option | One-line effect |
|----|--------|-----------------|
| A | Status quo | Keep the 1 July 2026 network: six withdrawals, thinned or retimed lines, Flex and the named substitutes, school-hour peaks kept. |
| B | Abolish / deregulate | Drop the duty to run a fixed line whose average maximum occupancy stays under the threshold De Lijn already uses. Let the transport region buy only the school and campus trips that have no alternative, from De Lijn or another operator, at a published euro per useful passenger. |
| C | Price / tax reform | Keep a social or student fare on the trips that matter, fund them by charging more where a car is the real alternative (parking and scarce road space), and stop paying for market extras and empty off-peak loops. |
| D | Conditional transfer | Restore or replace only trips with no workable substitute, against published boardings, and take the euro back after one school year if occupancy stays under the same threshold. |
| E | Public provision / invest | Put the withdrawn rural kilometres back and add frequency until access looks like the cities. This is ask 2 read without a cap. |
| F | Pilot | One school year, one corridor (Zwalm–Gent, or scrapped lijnen 408 and 427): restore or run flex on the school and campus trips, and publish cost per student boarding before any wider reopen. |
| G | **This proposal** | Review every cancellation and limit in the region, reinvest in rural access until chances match the cities, and give youth and local organisations a say. No line, no euro, no sunset. Plus an invitation to ride Zwalm–Gent. |

## 5. Distribution & transfer constraint

- Winners: Young people in the signing municipalities who lost a trip they used and cannot replace it with a car. Parents who now drive those trips. Local boards that said in February the sum of the cuts broke their mobility plan. Operators who would run the restored kilometres.
- Losers: Riders on lines that are actually full, if driver-hours move back to thin trips. Taxpayers who fund the return of kilometres De Lijn says were the least occupied. Anyone who gained from a change the letter would also “review,” including the Sunday extension on lijn 1 and the hourly extension of lijn 48.
- Exit / entry risk for productive base: A missing campus trip can push a student toward a kot, a car, or a nearer and narrower study choice. That is an entry cost into education and student jobs. Restoring empty loops does not create those jobs. An open-ended rural frequency standard can lock operating subsidy in a low-density network and make later entry of flex or another operator harder.
- New permanent clientele? Not a new agency. The risk is a standing claim, by municipalities and youth councils, on rural kilometres with no occupancy sunset. The vervoerregioraad had already organised against the parent saving. Youth councils are the messengers, not the fiscal client.

**capture_risk (0–10):** **4.0**

## 7. Belgian competence & implementation

- Competent level: Flemish. De Lijn is a Flemish public company. The mobility minister holds the dotation. Basisbereikbaarheid is the Flemish network frame the minister invoked in January. The vervoerregioraad advises and can propose alternatives. It does not adopt the budget. Youth councils have no spending competence. The letter is addressed to the right minister and skips the split between minister, operator and region council.
- EU constraint: No EU act forces these lines to stay or to go. Public-service contracts sit under the usual PSO frame. Nothing in the letter turns on an EU vote.
- Admin capacity / dual-structure friction: De Lijn told the regions the measures had to start on 1 July 2026 or the 2026 saving would cut deeper. Reopening in October, after implementation, is a second procedure on a timetable that already names substitutes and school-hour retiming. A youth seat can be added to the next region round. It does not, by itself, redraw 23 lines. No community (FWB) split: this is regional, not language-community, transport.

## 9. Recommendation

`amend`

Minimum fix: do not reopen all 23 changes. Publish, for each withdrawn or thinned trip that school and campus riders used, the H1 2025 occupancy and the alternative a person without a car actually has. Start with the Gent corridors the operator already named (lijnen 408 and 427) and any school trip whose substitute is a long walk. Restore or buy flex only for trips with no workable substitute, inside a euro the region states in public, and take that euro back after one school year if occupancy stays under the threshold De Lijn already uses. The youth seat feeds that list. It does not veto the rest of the package. The Zwalm–Gent ride is useful only if that corridor is one of the named trips.
