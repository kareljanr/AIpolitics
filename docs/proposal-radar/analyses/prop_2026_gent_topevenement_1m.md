## Steelman (proponent’s best case)

Ghent already chose, in the multi-year plan, to keep a city that still knows how to gather people. The preface of that plan says the new majority had to find a structural sanitisation of €120 million a year by 2028, about 12 percent of the net budget. Inside that contour the college still treats one large, free, low-threshold cultural event as part of what the city is for. The majority agreed that this council term there is room for one such event, on the scale of the Lichtfestival, and the 2029 budget still carries €1 million for it. That is a slower cadence than the old rhythm of every two years and then every three. It is one night-cycle of citywide art in six years, not a new annual festival.

The alderman’s case is that an event of that size does four jobs at once: culture, social mixing, tourism and local trade. He says the Lichtfestival brought hundreds of thousands of visitors, more than half of them from Ghent, and that few formats reach that many different people with no ticket and no language test. A free route through the streets is close to a public good until the streets congest. Residents who would never buy a concert ticket still walk into contemporary light art. Hotels, cafés and the evening economy get a winter weekend that the Gentse Feesten, which are in July, do not cover.

He also says the city will not send the tax bill to the ordinary budget. The event is to be paid from new income: higher auction proceeds for food stalls at the Gentse Feesten, and contributions from private organisers who use the public domain. Partners and sponsors are still to be found. The city is explicit that the concept is open. A new Lichtfestival remains “a valuable piste” for something with national and international reach. It is not yet a signed production contract. On this reading, €1 million is a ceiling and a signal to sponsors, small beside a €120 million structural repair, and the alternative is a city that saves its way out of the one format that still puts art in the street for free.

The timing objection, on the steelman, is backwards. The Flemish subsidy round and the inflation gap are exactly why the college wants one flagship that can raise its own income, rather than a stack of city-produced small events. Jazz in ’t Park is already no longer run by city services. Holding one line in 2029 is the disciplined version of the events policy, and dropping it now would tell sponsors there is nothing to finance.

## 1. Problem claim

- **Metric / baseline:** The operative claim on 12 October 2026 is qualitative: one event of this scale “kan een belangrijke culturele, maatschappelijke, toeristische en economische meerwaarde betekenen.” The only number attached to the instrument itself is a one-off €1 million in 2029. The predecessor the alderman points at is the Lichtfestival. This session could not re-download the city page that publicised 600,000 visitors for 31 January–4 February 2024 (HTTP 403). A 2024 council question, seen via a search extract, states about 600,000 visitors in 2024 and 810,000 in 2021. Those attendance figures are context for the predecessor, not a measured baseline for a 2029 concept that the college has not chosen. The adopted multi-year plan still says, in action plan OD20066, that the city organises a quality Lichtfestival every three years. The policy declaration (section 10.18) names “het Lichtfestival 2.0” as an international figurehead, with no euro amount and no year.
- **Who is hurt:** Residents who lose a free citywide format if the line disappears; the evening economy in the centre if a winter peak is cancelled; Ghent taxpayers if €1 million, plus unbooked policing and mobility, is spent while the city is inside a €120 million structural sanitisation and, on a separate press estimate by schepen Peeters, hunting a further amount “ruim 60 miljoen” after a Flemish subsidy cut and inflation. That €60 million figure is an alderman’s estimate in VRT, not a line in the plan.
- **Type:** preference (a free civic amenity people have used in large numbers), with a spillover claim dressed up as an economic necessity. It is also a transfer fight inside a savings round: which symbolic expenditure stays while the administration sheds functions.
- **Truth of problem diagnosis (0–10):** **4.0**
- **Confidence:** **Strong** that Ghent adopted a structural sanitisation of €120 million a year by 2028 (MJP preface). **Medium** that the college has publicly committed to one large 2029 event at €1 million (council question stating the announcement, plus two VRT articles). **Weak** on “meerwaarde”: no additionality test, no net-tax study, and the oral attendance line (“100.000-en”, “meer dan de helft uit Gent”) is coarser than the city’s own earlier counts and was not re-checked against a visitor study this session.
- **Sources:** MJP 2026-2031 PDF; council question 2026_MV_00723; VRT NWS 12 Oct 2026; VRT NWS 2 Dec 2025; VRT NWS 30 Sep 2026 for the separate €60 million estimate.

A free street festival that has drawn very large crowds is a real amenity. The diagnosis that Ghent needs to keep a €1 million reservation in 2029 in order to produce important economic added value, and that new income already pays for it, is not established.

## 2. Mechanism

Named mechanism(s):

1. **Public provision of a non-ticketed amenity.** One city envelope buys a single Lichtfestival-scale event in 2029 so access stays free and the format survives the savings round at a lower cadence.
2. **Spillover.** Cultural, social, tourist and trade “meerwaarde”, offered as the reason the envelope is an investment rather than consumption.
3. **User-charge offset.** Higher Gentse Feesten stall auctions and fees on private events on the public domain are supposed to fund the envelope, so the general municipal budget is held harmless.
4. **Sponsor leverage.** The city will look for partners and sponsors. The organiser may be the city, vzw Lichtfestival, or an external partner after an open call. That choice is not made.

Does the instrument actually attack that mechanism?

The reservation can buy (1) if a concept is chosen and the euro is actually appropriated to a production. As of 12 October 2026 the concept is explicitly open, almost a year after the announcement the question describes. Mechanism (2) is a hope. A gross visitor-spend figure, even if the 2024 evaluation’s “almost €30 million” and “€46.86 per visitor” are later confirmed, is turnover, much of it by residents who would have spent in Ghent anyway. The alderman’s own “more than half from Ghent” line, if it were accurate, would shrink the tourism increment. Mechanism (3) is a different instrument, and the adopted plan does not show it covering this event. Dienst Feesten en Ambulante Handel has expenditure of €9,880,731 in 2029 against €8,558,557 in 2028 and €8,858,013 in 2030. Receipts move only with the trend: €2,839,034 in 2029 against €2,783,398 in 2028, a rise of €55,636. The 2029 net cost of the service is €1,266,538 above 2028. Staffing in that service is 61.05 VTE in 2029 and 59.05 VTE in 2028 and in 2030. That is a one-year bump. It is not a receipt spike. The December 2025 press account had already assigned the same new income to a bundle: about €150,000 a year from private organisers “flowing back to free events as subsidies”, stall auctions aimed at about €450,000 of which about €150,000 would be incremental, €250,000 from equipment rental, and a separate €90,000 a year water event. The October 2026 article cites “ruim 200.000 euro meer” from the stalls, which does not match the December “zo’n 150.000 euro meer”. Neither figure equals €1 million, and neither is isolated for 2029 in the plan. Mechanism (4) is an intention. No sponsor contract is in the sources.

There is a second conflict inside the city’s own text. OD20066 still says the city organises a Lichtfestival every three years. The oral line is one event this term, in 2029, which may or may not be a Lichtfestival, so that a 2027 edition does not happen. The envelope and the strategic sentence are not yet the same instrument.

**mechanism_fit (0–10):** **3.5**

## 3. Options considered

| ID | Option | One-line effect |
|----|--------|-----------------|
| A | Status quo | Leave the 2029 feasts-service spike and the triennial sentence as printed in the multi-year plan, and leave the concept, the organiser and the funding story oral. |
| B | Abolish / deregulate | Delete the 2029 flagship envelope. Keep permits, the events desk and private use of the public domain. If a producer can finance a free or ticketed route with sponsors and stall or domain fees, the city facilitates and does not grant. |
| C | Price / tax reform | Charge the beneficiary: a modest visitor or hotel-night contribution in the festival window, or a domain fee that is booked on the same activity as the production cost. General taxation is not the residual payer by default. |
| D | Conditional transfer | Keep a small public floor for safety and open access only after a contracted private match. Claw back if the match fails. Stall-auction euros already promised to the Gentse Feesten and to other free events cannot be counted a second time. |
| E | Public provision / invest | Run one fully city-funded free edition, and publish the all-in cost, including policing and mobility, as the product. Drop the sentence that new income already pays for it. |
| F | Pilot | One 2029 edition only, with a pre-registered additionality and net-tax protocol (non-resident spend, hotel nights, domain fees, full public cost). The next council term gets the edition only if that protocol clears the cost. |
| G | **This proposal** | Hold about €1 million in the 2029 budget for one still-undefined flagship, comparable to the Lichtfestival, and say that new stall income and private-domain contributions will pay for it, while sponsors are sought. |

B is the clean abolish option: the city stops buying the spectacle and leaves the route to whoever will fund it. D is the version of G that would make the funding sentence true. G keeps the cheque first and the concept, the match and the cost boundary for later. A is what the documents currently do, which is not the same as G, because the plan still prints a triennial festival and an unlabelled 2029 spike.

## 5. Distribution & transfer constraint

- Winners: whoever is chosen to produce the 2029 event (city service, vzw Lichtfestival, or an external firm); centre hospitality; sponsors who buy association with a city route; residents who attend for free; the college’s “plezier” portfolio.
- Losers: municipal payers who fund the residual if the new-income story does not close; stallholders who pay higher auction prices that are then narrated as painless; organisers of other events who pay a new domain contribution that was already promised back to free events in general; neighbourhoods that absorb congestion, night noise and policing. Smaller city-produced events are already being pushed outward (Jazz in ’t Park no longer run by city services, per VRT).
- Exit / entry risk for productive base: low at €1 million. The risk is precedent and attention. A savings round that exempts the flagship teaches every other amenity to seek the same exemption. Higher stall and domain prices are a real cost to small traders and to volunteer organisers. They are not evidence that the general budget is untouched.
- New permanent clientele? Not a new agency. The MJP sentence that a Lichtfestival happens every three years is a franchise that renews. One “one-off” inside a triennial policy is how a clientele stays permanent while each edition is described as exceptional.

**capture_risk (0–10):** **6.0**

**fiscal_honesty (0–10):** **3.5**

The college is willing to say “€1 million”, and the adopted plan really does contain a one-year bump of that order in the responsible service, with two extra VTE that disappear again in 2030. That part is more honest than a slogan. The sentence that new income pays for the event is not what the receipt line shows. Receipts of the service rise by €55,636 in 2029, in line with the surrounding years. The press figures for incremental stall income (€150,000 in December 2025, “ruim €200,000” in October 2026) and for private-domain contributions (€150,000 a year) do not add up to €1 million, they conflict with each other, and they were already narrated as the funding of a wider bundle. Past editions, if the blocked evaluation document is right, cost €1.21 million (2021) and €1.59 million (2024) all-in. A €1 million ceiling can therefore also be a low headline over an all-in cost that includes security. The questioner asked whether €1 million is the whole budget and what estimate it rests on. The sources in hand do not contain the answer.

## 7. Belgian competence & implementation

- Competent level: the municipality. Events on the local public domain, the Gentse Feesten, and a city festival sit with the schepen van Plezier under the decree on local government. Flanders can subsidise tourism or culture on top. Nothing here requires a federal law.
- EU constraint: procurement. The question itself asks how an open call will be legally sound “gelet op de ervaring met de Winterfeesten”. That is a warning from the opposition, not a finding about a past tender. It is the right question to have on the file.
- Admin capacity / dual-structure friction: Ghent has produced this kind of event before, so capacity is real. Ten months after the announcement, the concept, the organiser and the council’s role in choosing both are still the questions on the agenda. The strategic note and the oral policy describe different calendars (every three years, versus once in 2029). Staffing shows a two-VTE hump in 2029 only, which is implementable and also easy to spend on coordination before a production exists.

**competence_fit (0–10):** **6.5**

## 9. Recommendation

`amend`

Minimum fix, before the 2027 budget adjustment:

1. Publish one 2029 activity that contains production, city staff, policing and mobility, and that subtracts only income contracted to that same activity.
2. Rewrite OD20066 so it matches the decision: either one 2029 edition, or the triennial festival the plan still prints. One sentence has to win.
3. If no organiser and no contracted non-tax match exist by that adjustment, release the 2029 bump back into the sanitisation.
4. Stop counting Gentse Feesten stall proceeds and domain fees, already tied to other free events, as if they also settle this €1 million.

Until that residual is published, the honest description is a city cheque. Option B (abolish the cheque, keep permits and private production) beats G. Option D is the amended G.
