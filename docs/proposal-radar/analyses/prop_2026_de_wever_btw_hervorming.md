## Steelman (proponent’s best case)

Belgium already taxes work very hard and consumption relatively lightly. The prime minister’s notes, as described by the press, try to use that gap instead of pretending another squeeze on wages, pensions, or health care is free. The sketch is a small step up in the standard rate, from 21% to 22%, a collapse of the 6% and 12% rates into one 9% rate, a zero rate on basic food such as fruit and vegetables, and an exception for new construction. The reported gross yield is €4.5 billion. The same account says that money would also pay for economic investment and for bringing forward and strengthening the labour-tax cut already promised to MR. If that second leg is real, this is a tax shift, not only a tax grab: the state taxes a broad, immobile base and gives part of it back on the margin that decides whether people and firms add hours and jobs.

That is the least bad large lever still on the table if the political target is an effort on the order of €10 billion by 2029. A broad base keeps the rate change small per purchase and is harder to dodge than a new levy on a mobile base. One reduced rate, instead of two, cuts the relative-price nonsense that Belgian VAT special cases create. The food zero rate is the social answer to the fact that poorer households spend more of their budget on consumption. Leaving new construction at the old standard rate is a bet that housing supply is rate-sensitive and employment-heavy. Last year’s narrower deal, a higher rate for takeaway and for sport and leisure, was so fiddly that nothing was collected, and the €700 million then booked still has to be found. A simple schedule is how you avoid repeating that camel.

The external case is the one Belgian governments have heard for years and, on this telling, still have not done: shift the burden from labour toward consumption. Experts quoted in the same debate (Peersman, Moesen, Van Craeynest) treat VAT as the least damaging place to look once unemployment and pensions have already been cut and health savings are already on the table. Moesen’s line, via Nieuwsblad, is that Belgian VAT yields about 6.1% of GDP against a European average of about 7.1%. De Wever’s own public warning is that partners who will not touch their taboos add to a debt that later costs real wealth, and his communiqué says a second year of provisional twelfths is not acceptable given the budget and the bond market. On the steelman, voting the rates down without a replacement is not prudence. It is a choice to keep the snowball.

## 1. Problem claim

- **Metric / baseline:** Press describes a federal effort “of at least €10 billion” by 2029, agreed in outline before summer 2026, still without a deal. VRT (10 Oct) says De Wever walked partners through National Bank and debt-agency figures in which the interest snowball starts in 2029 rather than 2031, or in 2028 if rates rise. VRT French (12 Oct) reports his Chamber warning that, at unchanged policy, the snowball can start in 2029 or earlier. None of those paths is an open budget table in this file.
- **Who is hurt:** Residents who will pay either higher future taxes, lower public services, or an inflation-and-index shock if consolidation is delayed; workers if the alternative is still more tax on labour; households, especially lower-income ones, if VAT rises and compensation does not arrive.
- **Type:** state failure (primary balance and debt dynamics) mixed with a tax-mix distortion (labour vs consumption). It is also a transfer fight inside the coalition about who pays.
- **Truth of problem diagnosis (0–10):** **7.5**
- **Confidence:** **Medium** that Belgium still needs a multi-billion consolidation and that the labour/consumption mix is skewed. **Weak** on the exact €10 billion path, the snowball year, and the claim that VAT is the only realistic instrument.
- **Sources:** VRT NWS 11 Oct 2026 (this unit); VRT NWS 10 Oct 2026; VRT NWS French / Belga 12 Oct 2026; Nieuwsblad 13 Oct 2026. No bill, no FPS Finance note, no National Bank slide.

The diagnosis of the *problem* is mostly right. The diagnosis that *this unpublished package* is the only way through it is not something the sources can carry.

## 2. Mechanism

Named mechanism(s):

1. **Tax-mix shift.** A broader consumption tax finances a lower tax on labour. That is the abundance mechanism, and it only exists if the labour-tax cut is in the same law as the VAT rise.
2. **Base broadening / rate simplification.** Merging 6% and 12% into 9% reduces arbitrary gaps between similar goods. A new zero rate and a new-build carve-out push the other way.
3. **Static consolidation.** Gross VAT yield improves the primary balance only to the extent it is not recycled into tax cuts, investment, or automatic indexation of wages and benefits.
4. **Belgian indexation leak.** A VAT rise lifts consumer prices. The health index then lifts wages and many benefits. Part of the gross yield comes back as higher public spending and higher labour cost. Size of that offset: **not published**. Existence of the channel: the indexation regime itself.

Does the instrument actually attack that mechanism?

The rate sketch attacks (1) and (2) **if** the Dutch press description is the note, and **if** the tax-cut leg survives. It attacks (3) only on the unrecycled remainder. It does not, in any source we have, attack (4). The nieuwbouw exception and the food zero rate are deliberate holes in an otherwise broad base. Unspecified “investeringen in de economie” are a spending mechanism wearing a tax-reform label.

A direct conflict remains on the reduced rate. Dutch VRT (10 Oct) and Nieuwsblad (13 Oct): 6% and 12% merge into **9%**. French VRT (12 Oct): 6% and 9% merge into **12%**. Those are different instruments. This memo scores the Dutch pair, because two newsrooms agree and Nieuwsblad adds the zero rate and the new-build exception, and it records the French sentence as unresolved.

**mechanism_fit (0–10):** **6.5**

## 3. Options considered

| ID | Option | One-line effect |
|----|--------|-----------------|
| A | Status quo | No VAT reform. Talks stay stuck. De Wever’s communiqué says a second year of provisional twelfths is not an option; the alternative in the press is a late deal or a political crisis. The debt path is unchanged. |
| B | Abolish / deregulate | Do not raise the standard rate. Abolish the reduced-rate maze (one rate, revenue-neutral) so relative prices stop being a lobby product. Kills the “camel” problem without booking a fictional yield. |
| C | Price / tax reform | Revenue-neutral shift: higher broad VAT, statutory labour-tax cut of equal static yield, no third use of the same euro. Closest clean version of the steelman. |
| D | Conditional transfer | Keep a VAT rise but compensate the bottom with a targeted transfer or refundable credit, instead of a new zero rate that everyone, including tourists and high incomes, receives on fruit and vegetables. |
| E | Public provision / invest | MR’s stated alternative: take the effort mostly in spending, already-decided measures, and growth, and cap new taxes near one-ninth (Clarinval: €1.2 billion of a €10 billion effort) without touching VAT. |
| F | Pilot | Legislate and actually collect last year’s narrow VAT base first. Publish the outturn against the €700 million that Nieuwsblad says was booked and not received. Only then scale. |
| G | **This proposal** | 21% → 22% (except new construction), 6% and 12% → 9%, zero rate on basic food, gross yield reported at €4.5 billion and also pledged to investment and an earlier labour-tax cut. Not tabled. MR’s answer, as of 13 Oct, still “non”. |

A and B are real. “Do nothing” leaves the snowball the prime minister describes. “Abolish the special cases” fixes distortion without pretending one receipt can fund consolidation, a tax cut, and an investment envelope.

## 5. Distribution & transfer constraint

- Winners: The treasury, on the unrecycled share. Workers and employers, if a statutory labour-tax cut of real size is enacted. Sectors that move from 12% toward 9%, if that is the note. Builders of new housing, if the nieuwbouw carve-out is real. Food shoppers, on a zero-rated basket.
- Losers: Households as consumers, especially lower-income ones, because they spend a larger share of income on taxed consumption (Sas, via Nieuwsblad: VAT is not the most social tax). Sectors moved up from 6% toward 9%. Exporters are largely unhurt (VAT is destination-based); import-competing and local services are not. Cross-border shoppers can shift some purchases. Public budgets and employers also lose if indexation lifts benefits and wages.
- Exit / entry risk for productive base: A pure labour-tax cut lowers the cost of adding a job. A VAT rise without that cut, amplified by indexation, raises it. The package as briefed contains both, so the sign of the labour-demand effect is not knowable from these articles. No quantified exit elasticity is cited here.
- New permanent clientele? The broad rate does not create one. The carve-outs do: construction (nieuwbouw exception), whoever defines the food zero rate, and whoever receives the “investeringen” slice. Those are permanent political claims on a tax expenditure, not a one-off.

**capture_risk (0–10):** **4.5**

Higher would be a subsidy with a named incumbent. This is lower than that because most of the money, if the base stays wide, comes from anonymous consumption. It is not low, because the brief already punches sectoral holes and an open investment use.

The direction “a broader VAT raises serious money” is honest. The arithmetic in the political use of that money is not. The same €4.5 billion cannot be the consolidation, the labour-tax cut, and the investment envelope. MR’s counter-claim, via VRT FR, is that the prime minister’s new table puts three to four times the agreed one-ninth on taxes in the early years. CSV fiscal amount columns are **blank on purpose**. Filling €4.5 billion as a cost would pretend households’ labour tax “funds” a subsidy. Filling it as a saving would pretend the gross is the net. Both would invent a figure the sources do not contain.

## 7. Belgian competence & implementation

- Competent level: **Federal.** VAT rates are a federal tax. Regions do not set them.
- EU constraint: The VAT Directive sets a floor under the standard rate (15%), so 22% is not the legal problem. Reduced rates and zero rates have to sit on the allowed list (the post-2022 Annex III flexibility, including some foodstuffs). Whether this food basket qualifies is a drafting question, not something these articles settle. Do not treat the zero rate as either safe or illegal.
- Admin capacity / dual-structure friction: FPS Finance can change a small number of rates. It already failed to operationalise a narrow, fiddly 2025 VAT tweak. A three-rate schedule (0 / 9 / 22) is administratively easier than that tweak and harder than “do nothing”. The binding constraint in October 2026 is coalition veto, not a second regional government. Indexation (federal benefits, and wage indexation via the social pipeline) is the implementation channel that can eat the yield without anyone voting an extra spending line.

**competence_fit (0–10):** **7.5**

Right level of government, implementable if the rate card stays short. Points come off for the zero-rate legal check and for a recent demonstration that a VAT “decision” can collect zero.

## 9. Recommendation

`amend`

Minimum fix before this can be scored as support:

1. Publish one static table in which gross VAT yield, the indexation clawback, the statutory labour-tax cut, the investment slice, and the residual primary balance are the same euros, not three posters.
2. Drop the nieuwbouw carve-out or publish it as a tax expenditure with a sunset. Keep at most one food zero rate, and only for a short list that the VAT Directive actually allows.
3. Do not book the gross as the budget effort. If MR’s one-ninth is the political constraint, show the net tax share after the labour-tax cut in the same article of the same law.
4. Do not repeat the 2025 pattern: no rate is “decided” until FPS Finance has an implementing text and a collection date.

If the labour-tax cut disappears and the law is only a price rise plus exemptions, the recommendation flips to **reject**.
