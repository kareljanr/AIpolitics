## Steelman (proponent’s best case)

Brussels has a shooting problem that is no longer a briefing slide. The regional government says that from 1 January to 30 September 2026 the capital counted 74 shootings, 2 people dead and 28 wounded, and that those incidents are tied to conflicts in the drug trade. People who live on the Antwerpsesteenweg, at Clemenceau, in Peterbos, at the North and South stations, or on Graystraat already know the sound. A minister-president who answers with another six-month photocopy of the last order is not answering.

The tactical claim is narrow and, if the count is real, useful. Between January 2025 and July 2026, the government says, an electric scooter showed up in more than a quarter of drug-related shootings, either as the platform the shot was fired from or as the way out. A scooter is quiet, quick in traffic, and easy to leave on a pavement. Shared scooters add a fleet that does not even belong to the user. Banning both riding and parking, private and shared, inside the 19 polygons takes that tool off the street where the trade is thickest. Shared fleets can be geofenced. A legal ban also gives a police officer a reason to stop a scooter in the sector without first proving a drug deal. Saint-Gilles mayor Jean Spinette, who has part of the South Station hotspot and the Hallepoort hotspot in his commune, put the operational version on the record the same day: the King’s prosecutor is asking for this, and it is an extra tool so officers can check every scooter user in those sectors.

The order is also a coordination device. Drug crews do not stop at a commune border. The minister-president’s administrative-police power exists for the case where the disturbance runs across municipalities, where a mayor does not act, or where the general interest requires one line. Dilliès’s public line is that everyone keeps their own job — mayors, zones, the prosecutor, prevention — and that the Region draws the course when the network ignores the map. One text, one list, a review every three months, a mayor who can ask to add or drop a quartier: that is how a temporary pile of extensions becomes something a zone can brief on Monday morning.

The rest of the order is the floor that already existed, kept on purpose. Do not walk into the zone for the sole purpose of buying drugs. No drinking on the public street. No takeaway alcohol between 22:00 and 06:00, with restaurants and bars left out. Identity checks, security searches and administrative seizures stay available around the clock. Fireworks used against police and emergency workers stay off the street across the Region. Selling nitrous oxide to the general public is closed, with a professional exception. When a hotspot tips into a serious threat, the mayor still has the heavier switch already used in places such as Peterbos: residents-only traffic, a ban on gatherings, night closure of businesses, a curfew, a local speed limit. Those are not everyday tools. They are the week the shooting does not stop.

The term is one year, in force from 15 November 2026 on the DH account of the announcement, replacing the habit of renewing the package every six months since the 2024 hotspot strategy. A year is long enough to see whether scooter-linked shootings inside the polygons fall, and short enough that the order dies unless someone defends it again. Dilliès’s closing line is the moral claim: no neighbourhood is to be left to the people shooting in the streets. A vehicle ban will not dismantle an import network. It can make the street tactic more expensive while the investigations catch up. That is the best case, and it is a real case.

## 1. Problem claim

- **Metric / baseline:** Two government figures, both carried by the press and neither published here as a table. (1) 74 shootings in Brussels from 1 January to 30 September 2026, 2 dead, 28 wounded, linked by the Region to drug-world conflicts (VRT, citing regional figures; DH states the same toll). (2) In more than a quarter of drug-related shootings from January 2025 to July 2026, an electric scooter was used to fire or to flee (VRT, BRUZZ, DH, Nieuwsblad, all attributing the sentence to the government or to Dilliès). No denominator, no definition of “used”, and no split between firing platform and escape was found in this pass. The hotspot list itself is older than this signature. A 2 March 2026 ministerial order in the Moniteur built the administrative-police frame and a shorter list. An 18 September 2026 amendment, published 2 October 2026, added the Gray quartier as point 19 on the list in the order of 2 September 2026. The 21 October order inherits that geography. BRUZZ places the 19 names across the City of Brussels, Saint-Gilles, Molenbeek, Anderlecht, Schaerbeek, Ixelles and Koekelberg, including both main railway stations.
- **Who is hurt:** People shot, their families, and residents of the labelled streets. Police and emergency workers hit with pyrotechnics. Legal scooter riders, including commuters, who lose the vehicle inside the polygon. Shopkeepers if a later night closure or curfew is switched on. The investigation system, if officers are pulled off cases to chase scooters.
- **Type:** state failure in the control of a violent street market (shootings, escape, cross-commune disorder), with a secondary mobility fight over who may ride in the labelled polygons.
- **Truth of problem diagnosis (0–10):** **7**
- **Confidence:** **Medium** that Brussels had a heavy run of shootings through the third quarter of 2026 and that the regional government is treating them as drug-linked. The toll is repeated by VRT and DH and is the sort of count a cabinet can be held to. **Weak** on the “more than a quarter” scooter share, because the press repeats a government sentence and does not show the coding rules. **Weak** on how much of the violence sits inside the 19 polygons rather than outside them. BRUZZ’s own later piece notes shootings and stabbings outside the known hotspots as well.
- **Sources:** VRT NWS 21 Oct 2026; BRUZZ 21 Oct 2026; DH 21 Oct 2026; Nieuwsblad 21 Oct 2026 (indexed cabinet line); Moniteur, order of 2 March 2026 (numac 2026001642) and amendment of 18 September 2026 (Staatsblad 2 Oct 2026).

## 2. Mechanism

Named mechanism(s):

1. **Tactic cost.** If shooters and runners actually depend on scooters, a ban on riding and parking inside the polygon raises the price of the approach and the getaway. Shared fleets can be switched off by the operator. Private scooters cannot.
2. **Stop authority.** A class ban lets police stop any scooter in the sector. Spinette states that use. The stop is the mechanism, not the impound by itself.
3. **Displacement.** Dealers and shooters can walk, use a bicycle or a car, or move two streets past the line. VUB criminologist Mattias De Backer expects that move and points to the South Station clean-up as the precedent. A hotspot list that can grow every three months is the government’s answer to displacement, and it is also how the exceptional zone spreads.
4. **Common frame.** One regional order, one list, a minister-president who can act when a mayor does not. That attacks a coordination failure. It does not attack wholesale supply, guns, or the expected sentence for pulling a trigger.
5. **Carried order-maintenance.** Alcohol hours, public drinking, intent-to-buy entry bans, and round-the-clock checks are loitering and nuisance tools. Pyrotechnics track a real weapon against police. Nitrous sales track a party drug more than a shooting. DH says the mayor’s emergency list, including the curfew, was already in the previous orders. Anderlecht mayor Fabrice Cumps says the same in substance: most of the text prolongs the old measures, and the real new measure is the scooter ban.

Does the instrument actually attack that mechanism?

The new piece attacks mechanism 1 only in part, and only if substitution is costly. The cabinet line reported by Nieuwsblad is that the ban binds everyone, including commuters. BRUZZ’s next-day note from the cabinet says there are no exceptions, the ban runs once the text is in the Staatsblad, and police will enforce it. A rule that prices the commuter and the shooter alike is a weak tax on the tactic and a broad tax on a legal vehicle. Cumps adds the practical hole: shared scooters can be cut off by technical means; private scooters are hard to announce, because the border of a hotspot is not readable on the pavement, and hard to police. De Backer adds the chase problem: stopping a fast scooter means catching it on the spot.

Mechanism 2 can work on the days officers are present. Spinette’s own caveat is that Brussels needs more officers and cannot keep lending the ones it has to other deployments. A stop power without a body to use it is a paragraph.

Mechanism 4 is the part that fits the minister-president’s job. Lengthening the term from six months to twelve is also the part Spinette flags as legally softer: the six-month habit existed in part so the liberty restriction stayed proportional, and a one-year order is easier to attack on that ground.

The curfew, the residents-only cordon and the night closures do not become a better fit for drug shootings by being repeated. They remain blunt tools for an acute week. VRT’s headline puts the curfew next to the scooter ban. DH and Cumps put the curfew in the inherited toolkit. The scored instrument includes both, and the weight of the novelty sits on the scooter rule.

**mechanism_fit (0–10):** **4**

## 3. Options considered

| ID | Option | One-line effect |
|----|--------|-----------------|
| A | Status quo | Keep renewing the pre-21 October hotspot orders every six months, without a class-wide scooter ban. |
| B | Abolish / deregulate | Let the regional hotspot order lapse and leave street order to ordinary municipal police regulations plus criminal investigations, with no ban on a vehicle class. |
| C | Price / tax reform | Drop the status ban. Fine and seize the scooter when it is used to deal, to carry a weapon, or to flee a shooting, and leave other riders alone. |
| D | Conditional transfer | Geofence shared scooters only, and only inside polygons that publish a scooter-linked shooting rate above a stated line; lift the geofence when the rate falls at the quarterly review. |
| E | Public provision / invest | Put the marginal police hour into investigations and local presence, which is the shortage Spinette names, and publish perimeters and a shooting map. |
| F | Pilot | Ninety days, two hotspots, shared scooters only, with a pre-registered inside-versus-outside shooting count and a count of stops of legal riders. |
| G | **This proposal** | The 21 October 2026 order as announced: private and shared scooters banned for riding and parking in all 19 hotspots, no exceptions, one-year term from 15 November 2026 on the DH account, carried alcohol and check rules, region-wide pyrotechnics and a nitrous sales ban except for professionals, quarterly mayor review, inherited emergency toolkit including a curfew. |

Option B is the real abolish case. The hotspot regime is a standing exceptional geography: special entry rules, special drink rules, special stops. Ending it returns those streets to ordinary police law and puts the entire weight on investigations, guns and sentences. That is coherent. It also drops the only regional lever that forces neighbouring mayors onto one list. Residents who wanted a visible street rule would get the criminal code and a municipal regulation instead. The mayors quoted here are not asking for that abolition. Spinette and Cumps argue about application, borders and proportionality. Schaerbeek acting mayor Martin de Brabant and Saint-Josse mayor Emir Kir, as reported via BRUZZ from Le Soir, are for the scooter ban. Opposition party Team Fouad Ahidar is against this version of it, because it does not separate riders who are dealing from riders who live there.

Option A is the live alternative the order replaces. The six-month renewals were clumsy and, on Spinette’s account, legally safer. They did not try to confiscate a whole vehicle class from commuters at the North and South stations.

Option G is what was signed. The amend path is D plus a published proportionality note for the twelve-month term, not a wider emergency toolkit.

## 5. Distribution & transfer constraint

- Winners: Residents if scooter-linked shootings inside the line actually fall and do not reappear on the next corner. Police officers who gain a simple stop ground. Shared-scooter operators only in the thin sense that a clear geofence is easier to obey than a vague request. Mayors who wanted one regional cover.
- Losers: Anyone who rides or parks an electric scooter in the 19 zones, including residents and station commuters. The cabinet position is that there is no exception. Shared operators who must keep fleets out of the polygons until the separate region-wide end of shared-scooter licences. Small businesses if a mayor uses the inherited night-closure power. People stopped under a round-the-clock check regime in a labelled neighbourhood.
- Exit / entry risk for productive base: A commuter ban at the North and South stations is a mobility tax on ordinary trips. It does not tax the drug margin. Productive activity leaves the polygon only if the label, the stops, or a curfew makes the street a place to avoid. That risk is local and real, and it is not an economy-wide exit.
- New permanent clientele? No new subsidy clientele and no new agency in this order. The clientele risk is geographic: 19 labelled zones, checks around the clock, and a list that moves when a mayor asks. A one-year term cuts the lock-in relative to a text with no end date. Moving from six months to twelve, for a liberty restriction, is still a step toward making the exception ordinary. Spinette says that step is where a proportionality challenge gets easier.

**capture_risk (0–10):** **4.5**

## 7. Belgian competence & implementation

- Competent level: The Brussels minister-president, for administrative police measures when disorder crosses communes or a mayor does not act. Drug crime, weapons and prosecution stay federal and with the prosecutor. The order sits on the correct shelf for street rules and on the wrong shelf if it is read as a drug-market reform. BRUZZ states that division of roles in the cabinet’s own words.
- EU constraint: No EU act is the operative limit in the sources. The live constraint is Belgian proportionality review. Spinette says measures that cut into liberties have to stay proportionate to the stated aim, and that stretching the term from six months to a year makes that attack easier. De Backer asks the same question of gathering bans. The Council of State is the path, not the Commission.
- Admin capacity / dual-structure friction: Nineteen polygons over many communes and more than one police zone. Perimeters are fixed, on the earlier Moniteur text, by a local police ordinance of the competent mayor. Cumps says those borders will not be obvious to a private rider. Safe.brussels and the communes are, on the cabinet note to BRUZZ, responsible for communication and handhaving. Spinette’s constraint is headcount and competing deployments. A rule that is easy to write for shared scooters and hard to apply to private ones will be applied unevenly, which is its own proportionality problem.

**competence_fit (0–10):** **6.5**

## 9. Recommendation

`amend`

Keep a one-year regional list and a geofence on shared scooters where a published scooter-linked shooting count clears a line that is written down in advance. Publish the perimeters before any private scooter is banned, and exempt residents and commuters unless the rider is stopped for a drug, weapons or flight offence. Keep the six-month proportionality logic, or publish the test that justifies twelve months. Leave the curfew and the residents-only cordon on the inherited, case-by-case footing, with a short maximum duration written into each use. Do not count this order as a substitute for the investigators Spinette asked for.

Minimum fix if only one change is available: drop the private-scooter ban until the borders are posted and the “more than a quarter” table is public.

## 10. Falsifier

> We reverse this assessment if, by 15 November 2027, a published police or prosecution series shows drug-related shootings inside the 19 perimeters down relative to the rest of the region and the drop is not absorbed by incidents just outside the polygons or by shootings that simply moved to other vehicles.

A second, sharper reverse toward reject: the same series is still unpublished at the first three-month review, or enforcement output is mainly legal riders while shootings inside the polygons are flat or higher.
