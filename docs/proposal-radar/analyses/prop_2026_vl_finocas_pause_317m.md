## Steelman (proponent’s best case)

Ghent’s steelworks is a real plant, not a brochure. The blast-furnace route is carbon-heavy, and the company has for years described a shift to direct-reduced iron and electric furnaces as the way to keep primary steel in Flanders. Flanders’ chosen vehicle is Finocas, a joint company with ArcelorMittal. The minister-president’s written answer is precise about the plumbing: both sides pay in capital, and Finocas then lends to ArcelorMittal Belgium to finance decarbonisation projects. Public money is matched, and it is supposed to come back as a loan rather than leave as a gift.

The company has not taken the big investment. In March 2025 it asked to rewrite the contracts around a phased path. The Flemish call that was planned for 2025, €140 million (half of a €280 million joint call), did not happen, because those talks were still open. The CEO’s later public line, reported by VRT, is that investing now would earn a negative return: weak demand for green steel, energy and staff costs, and European CO₂ levies. Paying the next slices on a calendar while the project sits still would park more taxpayer capital in a vehicle whose purpose is not being executed.

The October decision applies the rule Diependaele already stated to parliament: future calls should follow concrete milestones in the project, not fixed calendar years. VRT and Belga, both citing the cabinet, put the paused slices at €140 million in 2026 and €177.5 million in 2027, €317.5 million together. The engagement is not torn up. Flanders stays in Finocas, does not ask for the money already paid in, and, on the cabinet’s word via Belga, keeps a CO₂ cut of 44 percent by 2035 against 2018. The point of the pause is sequencing. Industry policy that wires cash to a hole in the ground is how hold-up works. Industry policy that waits for the hole is how a region keeps both the option and the cash.

A smaller step has continued. VRT reports that the Ghent site presented a new gas turbine about two weeks before this article, aimed at cutting about 10,000 tonnes of CO₂. That is phasing, which is what the company asked for, and the public calendar should phase with it. Unions that dislike blank cheques can live with this rule. The alternative, sending the 2026 and 2027 calls anyway, buys neither tonnes nor a final investment decision. It only buys a larger illiquid stake in a project the owner says is not yet worth building.

## 1. Problem claim

- **Metric / baseline:** Two nested claims. (1) Calendar capital calls would leave the Flemish budget while the decarbonisation project they are meant to finance is postponed. (2) Ghent steel is a large emissions and industrial-employment problem that a DRI-and-electric-furnace project was supposed to answer. On (1), the minister’s February 2026 answer is the baseline: Finocas capital stands at €700 million, of which €365 million is paid in, half by the Flemish Region and half by ArcelorMittal, and the 2025 Flemish call of €140 million did not take place. On (2), VRT says the plant is 4 percent of Flemish greenhouse-gas emissions and that the company announced a €2 billion project in 2021. This session did not match the 4 percent figure to the VMM inventory. Statistiek Vlaanderen puts total Flemish greenhouse-gas emissions at 66.4 Mton CO₂-equivalent in 2024; that regional total is not a plant total.
- **Who is hurt:** Taxpayers, if further calls are paid into a loan vehicle before the investment exists and if those calls hit the budget (the parliamentary question asserts they are not ESR-neutral; the answer does not confirm that). Workers and suppliers in Ghent, if the plant’s long-run process route is abandoned. Residents exposed to the site’s air emissions, which VMM treats as an industrial source in the Ghent canal zone for NOx and fine dust; that is local air quality, a different ledger from the CO₂ project.
- **Type:** state failure on the disbursement rule (a calendar call that ignores whether the project is being built), sitting on top of a market-cost problem (the company says green steel does not pay yet). Also a transfer fight over a single-firm vehicle.
- **Truth of problem diagnosis (0–10):** **7.5**
- **Confidence:** **Strong** that the 2025 call of €140 million did not happen and that Flanders, as of the February answer, wanted milestones instead of calendar years. **Medium** that the company has deferred the large DRI and electric-furnace decision (VRT, plus the minister’s account of the March 2025 request). **Weak** on “4 percent of Flemish emissions”, the 2021 €2 billion headline, and the cabinet’s “44 percent by 2035 versus 2018”, which Belga reports and no decision text here restates.
- **Sources:** Vlaams Parlement, answer to question 236 of 10 Feb 2026; VRT NWS 13 Oct 2026; La Libre / Belga 13 Oct 2026; Statistiek Vlaanderen greenhouse-gas page (regional total only).

## 2. Mechanism

Named mechanism(s): **conditional transfer** inside an existing **capital-to-loan** vehicle. Public equity into Finocas is the funding pipe; the loan to ArcelorMittal Belgium is the economic instrument; the October act is a **stop on the next calendar calls** until concrete steps exist.

Does the instrument actually attack that mechanism?

The failure mode being scored is “pay on a date even if the asset is not being built.” Withholding the next calls attacks that failure directly. It does not attack the reasons the company gave for a negative return: power and gas prices, the price of green hydrogen, labour cost, demand for a green-steel premium, and the CO₂-cost stack. A pause cannot make an unprofitable furnace profitable. It can refuse to finance one before the private decision.

The mechanism is only half-built. The February answer said the contractual talks were still under way. Belga says the authorities have not published a new calendar or the conditions for releasing the money. A principle without a milestone table (which physical step, which euro, what private match, what happens if the step never comes) is a press sentence. The stock of capital already paid in is untouched: the answer says a capital reduction is not being considered, and Belga says Flanders will not reclaim amounts already invested. For money already inside Finocas, “funds follow concrete steps” is not yet the rule.

**mechanism_fit (0–10):** **7.5**

## 3. Options considered

| ID | Option | One-line effect |
|----|--------|-----------------|
| A | Status quo | Pay the next Finocas calls on the calendar even though the large project is postponed. |
| B | Abolish / deregulate | Stop new calls, reduce idle paid-in capital, and let the company finance any later furnace on market terms or under a general EU instrument open to every firm. |
| C | Price / tax reform | Leave Finocas alone and move the cost stack that the company cites: power price, the labour-tax wedge, permitting, and a carbon price that is predictable for trade-exposed steel. |
| D | Conditional transfer | Publish the milestone contract: euro per met physical step, private match, clawback, and a date after which unused authority expires. |
| E | Public provision / invest | Flanders builds or co-owns the DRI and electric furnaces itself. Shifts construction and technology risk onto the taxpayer. |
| F | Pilot | Release only a small, audited tranche against one defined package (for example the turbine or a named first DRI package) and publish the cost per tonne before any further call. |
| G | **This proposal** | Pause the reported 2026 and 2027 calls (€140 million and €177.5 million), keep the engagement and the paid-in stake, and do not yet publish the milestones or a sunset. |

A is the path this decision refuses, and that refusal is the sound part. B is the clean long-run rule if the region does not want a single-firm soft loan on the books; it gives up the option to co-finance a later investment at below-market terms. C is the abundance policy that would change the NPV the CEO described; it is slower than a call schedule and it is mostly federal and European, not a Finocas decision. D is this proposal with the missing contract. E is dominated by D for a private site. F is the honest way to fund the “phased” path the company requested. G is D announced and not yet written down, plus an explicit refusal to touch the stock.

## 5. Distribution & transfer constraint

- Winners: Flemish taxpayers, for as long as the €317.5 million is actually not called. Other industrial firms, in the weak sense that one competitor does not receive the next below-market loan while they do not. The cabinet, which can say it did not hand out a blank cheque.
- Losers: ArcelorMittal, which loses a near-term capital call it was not, on the CEO’s own words, ready to match with the big project. Ghent’s steel workforce, only if a call that will not be paid was in fact the margin between staying and leaving. Nothing in the sources shows that the 2026–27 calls were that margin. The company postponed the project before this pause.
- Exit / entry risk for productive base: The plant’s location economics are the energy, labour and carbon stack, which this pause does not change. Holding an exclusive Finocas option can delay exit; it can also teach every other large site to wait for a public milestone cheque. Entry of competing steel or of other decarbonisation routes is not helped by a single-counterparty vehicle.
- New permanent clientele? The clientele already exists. The pause does not create it. Keeping the earmark and refusing a capital reduction maintains it.

**capture_risk (0–10):** **7.0**

## 7. Belgian competence & implementation

- Competent level: Flanders. The participation sits with the Flemish Region; the announcing minister is the minister-president, who also holds economy, innovation and industry. A Finocas call is a regional capital decision, not a federal tax.
- EU constraint: A loan to one steel group on softer-than-market terms is state aid. The 2024 basisrapport extract points to a Commission authorisation of 22 June 2023, capped, for RecHycle including DRI. Later capital increases still have to fit that decision or a new one. Pausing a call is the legally easy direction. Paying one outside the authorised project would not be.
- Admin capacity / dual-structure friction: Finocas is 50/50. The Region cannot unilaterally aim the cash once it is paid in, which is the point of Lamont’s question. Implementation of *this* decision is a matter of not signing the next volstorting and of finishing the contract the February answer said was still open. That is within Flemish capacity. Publishing the milestone table is the part that has not happened.

**competence_fit (0–10):** **7.5**

## 9. Recommendation

`amend`

Minimum fix, before any further euro moves:

1. Publish the contract table: physical milestone, private euro that must already be spent or committed, Flemish call released, and the audit who checks it.
2. State the aid element separately from the gross capital call, and point to the Commission decision that covers it.
3. Put a sunset on unused authority. If no final investment decision meets the table by a named date, the uncalled slices die and idle paid-in capital can be reduced. “Engagement unchanged” without a date is an option with no price.
4. Do not pay the next call on a calendar in the meantime.
