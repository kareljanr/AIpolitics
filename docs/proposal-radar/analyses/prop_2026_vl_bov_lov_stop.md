## Steelman (proponent’s best case)

BOV was a project subsidy, not a right. It was approved at the end of 2015, rolled out after local pilots, and extended so that it would end with the health-goal period. By late 2025 the fee-for-service had outgrown the envelope: the operator itself stopped encouraging new trajectories and put quotas on coaches so that invoices would still be paid. A scheme that adds 48 percent more intakes in a single year (3,719 in 2024) and then hits a cash ceiling is not yet a sustainable instrument.

The administration had already drafted a successor, Leefstijl op Verwijzing, with a published call ceiling of €1,620,000 a year (forfait up to €870,000, variable coaching up to €750,000). That design was a cap, a narrower referral rule, and more group work, not an open cheque. When the autumn 2026 Flemish budget agreement required every domain to save, declining to sign the new management agreement was a clean stop. It does not touch disability budgets, home nursing, or the Groeipakket. It does not ban movement, sport clubs, municipal programmes, or a GP’s advice. It ends a public tariff of €11.75 to €15.75 per 15 minutes of motivational coaching, plus a coordination layer, bought against an intermediate outcome (self-reported activity), not against a measured drop in diabetes or admissions.

About 150 coaches and a few thousand new participants a year is a real service and a small one. In a region of roughly 6.8 million people, not renewing it is a modest consolidation line with a fully describable static cost, at a moment when the same welfare budget is being asked to contribute to a multi-billion Flemish exercise. People who want coaching can still buy it. The public purse stops buying it for them.

## 1. Problem claim

- **Metric / baseline:** Two different problems are in play. The budget problem is that the 2026 BOV line is €1,109,070 and already needed rationing in November–December 2025. The health problem, which the minister stated in parliament, is inactivity: after a BOV trajectory participants “move twice as much”, and in 2024 four in ten coaching contacts were with someone entitled to the verhoogde tegemoetkoming (VT). The October decision treats the first problem as sufficient reason to drop the second.
- **Who is hurt:** Today, adults in Flanders or Brussels who are referred because they sit too much or miss the activity guideline, and who pay €0.50–€5 per 15 minutes only because the Flemish tariff covers the rest. In 2025 the operator says 42 percent of participants had a VT status. Coaches and the coordinating vzw lose the subsidy. The Flemish treasury is the party the cut relieves.
- **Type:** state failure (a project subsidy that ratcheted past its envelope) mixed with a possible market failure (activity has health externalities that a private coaching price will under-supply, especially for low-income adults). It is also a transfer fight between a small professional network and the budget.
- **Truth of problem diagnosis (0–10):** 6
- **Confidence:** Medium on the budget overrun and the reach figures the minister put in parliament. Weak on any claim that ending the line leaves disease incidence unchanged.
- **Sources:** Flemish Parliament, answer to Schryvers question 1073 (evaluation and the promise of a 2027 framework); answer to Van den Driessche question 1155 (2026 cost table); Gezond Leven jaarrapport 2025 (quotas); Gezond Leven 8 Oct 2026 (the stop).

The diagnosis that “this line is fat” is half right. The fee-for-service did hit the ceiling. The diagnosis that the activity result can be discarded without a replacement is not supported by the minister’s own earlier answer.

## 2. Mechanism

Named mechanism(s): subsidy expiry; price of coaching for the referred adult; intergovernmental externality (Flanders books the saving, federal compulsory health insurance carries most of the treatment bill if cardiometabolic disease rises).

BOV works as a referred, subsidised coaching episode. A care professional writes a referral. The adult gets 84 points over two calendar years. Individual coaching costs the user at most €5 per 15 minutes (€1 with VT); the Flemish intervention on that quarter-hour is €11.75 (ordinary) or €15.75 (VT). Group quarters are cheaper (public €5.75 or €6.25). The coach does not exercise with the person. The coach writes a plan and follows up. The public good, if any, is sustained extra activity and, further downstream, fewer avoidable cases of diabetes, cardiovascular disease and related disability.

Does the October instrument attack that mechanism? It attacks the subsidy, completely. From 1 Jan 2027 there is no Flemish tariff and no LOV. It does not retarget the subsidy to the cardiometabolic risk the successor call was written for. It does not raise the co-pay and keep a cap. It does not test whether the late-2025 quota, rather than abolition, was the right brake on the ratchet. It also does nothing about the federal price of treating the diseases the activity was meant to delay. That last gap is the content of the headline metaphor: a small Flemish saving now, an unmeasured treatment bill later. The metaphor is a mechanism sketch, not a measured euro.

**mechanism_fit (0–10):** 5

The stop matches a budget-cut goal. It does not match the health goal the same minister described to parliament in the answer to question 1073 (“from 2027 a durable framework for lifestyle-on-referral”) or the capped redesign already in the call. Fit is partial.

## 3. Options considered

| ID | Option | One-line effect |
|----|--------|-----------------|
| A | Status quo | Roll the 2026 BOV budget (€1,109,070) into 2027 and keep open referral. Protects the pathway and keeps the fee-for-service ratchet. |
| B | Abolish / deregulate | Let the subsidy expire, sign no LOV, do not renew the OGIP coordination agreement. Coaching only at private, municipal or mutuality prices. This is the decision. |
| C | Price / tax reform | Keep the pathway. Raise the ordinary co-pay, leave the VT tariff, and cap consults per coach so the public price stops overrunning the envelope. No new tax. |
| D | Conditional transfer | Voucher only for VT and/or a documented cardiometabolic risk, volume-capped at the call’s €750,000 variable slice. |
| E | Public provision / invest | Sign LOV at the published call ceiling and pay for a pre-registered outcome study. Spends up to €1.62m a year on a consolidated diet-and-movement referral. |
| F | Pilot | One set of municipalities against matched controls for 24 months, with activity and care-use endpoints, before any Flanders-wide stop. |
| G | **This proposal** | Full stop of BOV and of LOV from 1 Jan 2027, plus non-renewal of OGIP, as the welfare contribution to the budget agreement. No replacement pathway. |

Option B/G is what was announced. Option D is the instrument the call had already started to draw: a ceiling, a narrower target, group sessions instead of an open individual tariff. Abolition was not the only cut available.

## 5. Distribution & transfer constraint

- Winners: The Flemish budget, for a static amount on the order of €1.1–1.6 million a year from 2027. That is small against a multi-billion consolidation. Per employee it is a fraction of a minute of gross pay (see pain: the sign is a relief).
- Losers: Adults who would have been referred from 2027, especially VT households for whom the public tariff was €15.75 of a €16.75 quarter-hour. About 150 coaches lose the subsidised tariff. Gezond Leven loses the coordination subsidy and has said twelve jobs go in the wider prevention cut. Referrers lose a pathway. Mutuality rebates tied to the scheme (Helan stated its own €25 rebate disappears with the Flemish stop) shrink with it.
- Exit / entry risk for productive base: Low. This is not a tax on work or on firms. A thin coaching niche loses a public tariff and may exit or go private. Municipal top-ups (Genk made coaching free on top of the Flemish tariff) lose their base.
- New permanent clientele? No. The cut shrinks one. The capture that remains is political: a visible prevention cut can be booked while larger, less evaluated welfare agreements continue.

**capture_risk (0–10):** 3.5

Ending the tariff reduces a subsidised network. It does not create a new bureau. Residual risk is that the “saving” is the photogenic line, not the tested one.

## 7. Belgian competence & implementation

- Competent level: Preventive health promotion is Flemish, under the Preventiedecreet. The minister can let a Flemish project subsidy expire. Domicile rule of the scheme also covered Brussels residents; the payer was still Flanders.
- EU constraint: None identified for ending this subsidy.
- Admin capacity / dual-structure friction: Stopping is easy. The hard part is the split with federal compulsory health insurance, which pays most of the treatment cost of diabetes and cardiovascular disease, and with mutualities and municipalities that built rebates on the Flemish tariff. Those actors were not part of the decision, on the sources opened here.

**competence_fit (0–10):** 7.5

Right level, easy to execute. The missing piece is coordination with the level of government that pays the downstream bill, not a lack of legal power.

## 9. Recommendation

`amend`

Minimum fix: do not zero the pathway. Keep a 2027 agreement inside the call ceiling of €1,620,000, and spend the scarce money on the variable coaching slice for VT adults and documented cardiometabolic risk rather than on the larger forfait. Cap volumes, which is what the late-2025 quota already did in panic form. Publish the evaluation behind “they move twice as much” with a control or a matched cohort before any later abolition. Option D above is the concrete version.
