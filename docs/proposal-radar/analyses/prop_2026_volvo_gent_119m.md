## Executive judgment (fair)

This is **not** Smaakhaven-class prestige fluff. Volvo Car Gent is a real plant (~6,300–6,500 people; 212k cars in 2025), Belgium’s last mass car assembly site, and a material node in Flanders’ industrial wage base. The offer is framed as **conditional** investment aid (training, energy efficiency, innovation)—closer to EU-allowed categories than a pure operating loss subsidy.

It is also **not** genius industrial strategy. €119m does not fix Belgium’s structural labour-tax wedge, energy prices, or indexation dynamics that the company itself flags as “too expensive.” Global location choices (Slovakia megacasting, Geely platform allocation, possible Spanish capacity) will dominate. The politics of “fabriek is gered” oversells an MoU. Fair score: **mixed-to-poor on incentives**, **serious on the problem**, **second-best instrument**.

## Steelman (extensive, good faith)

### 1. The plant is a real productive asset

- Volvo Car Gent: ~**6,300–6,500** employees; largest industrial employer class in Flanders (company site).  
- **212,177** vehicles built in **2025** (+13.9% YoY per company). Models include XC40/EX40/EC40, V60, EX30 EV line.  
- Shutdown would destroy specific human capital, supplier tiers (Agoria: auto industry ~450 firms / ~42k jobs class — sector-wide, not all Volvo-dependent), and municipal/regional tax base. Social insurance costs of mass redundancy are real public euros too.

### 2. Competitiveness pressure is not invented

- CEO messaging (reported): Belgian site **too expensive** vs alternatives.  
- New Volvo capacity in **Košice (Slovakia)** with lower wage costs and **megacasting** (large aluminium structural castings) that Ghent lacks; next-gen EVs need that capability (secondary industry analysis — **Medium**).  
- Installing megacasting is **hundreds of millions** of private capex; public sweetener can tip marginal investment decisions.  
- Energy costs for heavy industry in BE are a documented headache; €30m green/efficiency pot maps to a real cost line.

### 3. Conditional design is better than a blank cheque

Official Flanders framing (Diependaele, VRT 6 Jun): **“geen blanco cheque”** — support only when investments **actually happen**. Package split:

| Bucket | Ceiling | Logic (steelman) |
|--------|--------:|------------------|
| Energy efficiency / circular | **€30m** | Cuts unit energy cost; climate co-benefit |
| Innovation (batteries, digitalisation, advanced manufacturing) | **€80m / 10y** | Aligns with EV transition, EU RDI aid norms |
| Training | **€9m** | Skills for new processes; workers not left behind |

EU state-aid law constrains pure gifts; investment- and training-linked aid is the legal corridor governments actually use.

### 4. Strategic upside beyond “save Volvo badge”

- MoU language: possible assembly of **other Geely-group brands** (Polestar, Lynk & Co, Zeekr class) → utilisation if volumes appear (tariff / local-content logic for Chinese-linked groups in EU).  
- De Wever narrative: EV hub, multi-decade horizon, “maakindustrie niet verloren.”  
- Multiplier: logistics (Ghent port/hinterland), tooling suppliers, local services.

### 5. Counterfactual may be worse

If Gothenburg/Geely were close to cutting Ghent, **doing nothing** could mean 6k+ direct jobs + suppliers lost for a fiscal saving of €0–119m that is tiny vs social cost. Horizontal reform (tax wedge) is first-best but **slow**; plant decisions are **now**. Second-best aid can be rational under political time inconsistency.

### 6. Political economy of last plant

Voters and unions correctly treat the “last car factory” as national industrial identity. A transparent conditional package can be preferable to panic nationalisation or endless crisis PR.

## Critique (extensive, still fair)

### 1. Problem claim — graded

| Claim | Grade | Note |
|-------|-------|------|
| Ghent faces cost competitiveness vs CEE/Asia-linked sites | **Medium–Strong** | Company + industry press; wage/energy/indexation known BE weaknesses |
| Without €119m plant closes soon | **Weak–Speculative** | MoU after “months of talks”; 2025 production **rose**; threat may be bargaining |
| €119m secures “decades” of capacity | **Weak** | Rhetoric (De Wever); history of BE auto sites says otherwise |
| Training/green/innovation pots fix root costs | **Weak** | Structural tax wedge & energy policy dominate unit labour/energy costs |

**truth_problem ≈ 7.0** for “BE manufacturing cost problem,” **not** for “this cheque solves it.”

### 2. Mechanism fit — partial

| Intended mechanism | Fit |
|--------------------|-----|
| Lower effective investment cost → private capex in Ghent | Medium if clawbacks bind |
| Lower energy intensity → ongoing unit cost | Medium for €30m slice only |
| Skills → productivity | Medium if training is real, not payroll padding |
| Change Geely global allocation | **Weak** — strategic HQ decision |
| Fix wage indexation / labour tax wedge | **None** |

Root problem is **location economics** (wages, energy, capital intensity, platform strategy). Firm-specific subsidies are a **patch**, not a mechanism repair. They also create **hold-up**: every multi-national learns to threaten exit before investing.

### 3. Additionality & history (Belgium’s auto scar tissue)

Industry commentary (newmobility and others) correctly lists prior supported sites that still closed or shrank: **Ford Genk**, **Opel Antwerp**, **Renault Vilvoorde**, **Audi Brussels**. Ford Genk even faced clawback/repayment politics after closure. Pattern: subsidies **buy time**; they do not rewrite comparative advantage.

Fair reading: past failure does **not** prove *this* package fails — each case differs — but it **raises the prior** that “fabriek gered” headlines are premature.

### 4. Scale mismatch

- Public ceiling: **€119m**.  
- Private modernisation often described in **€1bn class** problem/opportunity language (De Tijd taskforce context).  
- Implication A: public money is a **symbolic tip** that doesn’t change the investment NPV.  
- Implication B: public money is the **political fig leaf** Geely/Volvo needed for a decision already leaning Ghent.  
- Implication C: it unlocks a **specific** missing piece (e.g. megacasting share).  

Without a published investment schedule and matching private €, we cannot distinguish A/B/C. **Fiscal honesty suffers**.

### 5. “Not a gift” vs political speech

| Message | Reality check |
|---------|----------------|
| Geen blanco cheque | Good — conditionality stated |
| Steun pas bij investeringen | Good if audited |
| “Fabriek is gered” / groei / decennia | **Oversell** for an MoU |
| Knowhow as decisive | Soft factor; hard costs dominate |

Fairness: politicians must sell wins; analysts must separate **MoU** from **cash disbursed** and from **volume guaranteed**.

### 6. EU / sister-brand scenario cuts both ways

- **Upside:** multi-brand utilisation raises load factor.  
- **Downside:** Geely may prefer **other EU sites** (e.g. rumoured Spanish capacity) for some models; Ghent is one bargaining chip among many.  
- Tariff-driven localisation is real EU politics — but it does not obligate *this* plant or *this* subsidy level.

### 7. Opportunity cost (abundance lens)

€119m ceiling could instead fund (illustrative, not scored as policy):

- Broad **labour tax wedge** cut infinitesimal but horizontal  
- Energy network / permitting acceleration for *all* manufacturers  
- Portable training vouchers not locked to one employer  

Firm-specific aid fails the “same treatment for competitors” test and invites the next lobby (chemicals, pharma, steel).

### 8. Distribution & capture

| Winners | Losers |
|---------|--------|
| Volvo/Geely (if aid is free option) | General taxpayers (6k Belasting-FTE class) |
| Ghent workers if plant stays & volumes hold | Other BE manufacturers without equal package |
| Local suppliers if utilisation up | Future coalitions locked into “save every plant” path |
| Incumbent politicians (credit claim) | |

**capture_risk high (7.5)** but not cartoonish: workers’ interests are legitimate; the issue is instrument.

### 9. Options

| ID | Option | Comment |
|----|--------|---------|
| A | Do nothing / pure market | Risk real job loss; first-best on incentives if threat is bluff |
| B | **Abolish firm aid; only horizontal reforms** | Best long-run abundance; slow for 2026 decision |
| C | Conditional investment aid (this package class) | Second-best; design quality is everything |
| D | Equity stake / nationalisation cosplay | Worse incentives |
| E | Wage subsidies forever | Operating aid; high clown |
| F | Pilot: publish CBA + hard clawbacks + private match ≥X:1 | Upgrade path |

**This proposal is C.** Score depends on moving C toward F.

## Recommendation

**`amend`** (not blanket reject, not cheerleading support).

Minimum upgrades before celebrating:

1. **Publish** the MoU annexes that matter: private investment € schedule, employment floors, clawback triggers, state-aid legal basis.  
2. **Hard clawbacks** if volumes/employment fall below thresholds within N years (learn from Genk politics).  
3. **Matching ratio** — public € only as minority of documented private capex.  
4. **Parallel structural work** (energy, labour cost, permitting) so the next OEM doesn’t queue the same ransom.  
5. **No operating-loss top-ups** later sold as “innovation.”  
6. Track sister-brand volume as a **KPI**, not a hope.

Default abundance ranking still prefers **horizontal** competitiveness over **firm-specific** aid — but given last-plant stakes, a **tightly clawed investment package** can be second-best **if** additionality is real.

## Falsifier

> We move toward **support** if within 5 years of first disbursement: (i) private capex ≥ 5× public cash, (ii) employment and vehicle volumes at or above MoU floors, (iii) no follow-on operating aid, and (iv) independent eval finds high additionality (investment would not have occurred in BE at similar scale).  
> We move toward **reject / stop further pay** if private match fails, employment erodes while aid is paid, or aid is re-labelled operating support.

## Bottom line (plain language)

**Fair version:** Saving Belgium’s last car plant is a legitimate public concern. A **conditional**, investment-tied package for training, energy efficiency and RDI is more defensible than free cash for losses. The people and the production numbers are real.

**Honest version:** €119m does not rewrite Belgian unit costs or Geely’s European map. Politicians declared the factory “saved” on the back of an MoU. History of auto aid in Belgium is sobering. Without hard private match, clawbacks and structural reform, this is **expensive insurance against a threat that may be partly strategic bargaining** — about **50 minutes of work from every Belgian employee** at the full ceiling, or **~6,100 full annual labour-tax bills**.

Not a clown special. Not genius. **Amend hard, then verify.**
